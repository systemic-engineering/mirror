## §1 The @gift family-root substrate-decl

### 1.1 What @gift is (structural claim)

@gift is the substrate-decl carrier for **attribution-preserving-transfer**.
An @gift is a triple relation — giver, receiver, artifact — with FOUR
structural invariants:

1. **Attribution preserved forever.** The giver's identity is a load-
   bearing byte in every downstream composition of the gifted artifact.
   Removing the attribution IS removing the gift — the resulting
   artifact is a different substrate object with different lineage.
2. **Use-rights transferred.** The receiver may compose the artifact
   freely within the substrate; the giver does not gate downstream use.
3. **No reciprocity expected.** The gift does not create a transactional
   obligation. The receiver's future actions are not owed to the giver.
4. **Declinable acceptance (ADO).** The receiver retains the sovereign
   right to decline. The gift is not offered; it is accepted. Non-
   acceptance is structurally admissible and does not diminish the
   giver.

The fifth invariant, at composition altitude:

5. **Composition-honest.** Downstream compositions that consume the
   gift MUST surface the attribution in their own lineage. The substrate
   enforces this via content-addressing (per @kintsugi/store/git): every
   composition that reads a gifted artifact reads the giver's identity
   as byte-visible provenance.

### 1.2 Why @gift is structurally distinct from every landed carrier

Reed's framing (§0.3, verbatim) names five carriers @gift is NOT. Each
distinction is load-bearing at substrate-decl altitude:

- **@gift ≠ @consent (transactional agreement).** @consent is a two-
  sided negotiation over a proposed morphism; both parties adjudicate;
  the outcome is Pass | Partial | Fail per @kintsugi/consent's three-
  state floor. @gift is one-sided at offer-time (the giver acts
  unilaterally); the receiver's role is only to accept or decline; there
  is no negotiation over the artifact's shape.
- **@gift ≠ @io (exchange).** @io is a byte-crossing at the substrate's
  Turing-unbounded wall (per Recognition #79 `glass_wall`). @io is
  content-transportation across the substrate/non-substrate boundary.
  @gift is content-attribution WITHIN the substrate's gauge-bounded
  interior, or from a @subject at the boundary INTO the interior with
  attribution preserved. @gift may cross @io (§4.7), but the crossing
  is distinct from the giving.
- **@gift ≠ @peer (reciprocity-expecting).** @peer is a Pack-
  coordination role with reciprocal observation obligations (peers
  observe each other's outputs; consent-adjudicate; commit-as-fold).
  @gift explicitly REFUSES reciprocity expectation; the giver does not
  retain a peering claim on the receiver.
- **@gift ≠ @kintsugi (mending-broken).** @kintsugi is the mending
  discipline: the substrate detects a fracture, offers gold, mends. The
  gold IS gift-adjacent (see §4.4: kintsugi mends WITH a gift of gold)
  but @kintsugi as family-root is the mending PROCESS, not the gift
  TYPE. @gift as family-root names the type of the gold @kintsugi
  offers.
- **@gift ≠ @subject (licensable-party).** @subject is the substrate-
  external licensable party (the person the substrate observes, uses
  labor from, or acts upon; SEL Downstream User / Witnessed /
  labor_input). @gift is a RELATION between @subjects (or between a
  @subject and the substrate); @gift is not itself a party. The
  giver:@subject and receiver:@subject fields make the party-carriers
  explicit.

Absence of an existing carrier for the shape is not the argument for
minting @gift. The argument is that the shape is LOAD-BEARING —
downstream compositions need to reason about attribution-preserving-
transfer as a first-class substrate concept, and no landed carrier
supports that reasoning at substrate-decl altitude.

### 1.3 The anti-extraction structural claim

@gift makes the giver STRUCTURALLY VISIBLE at every downstream use. This
is not a policy — it is a substrate-decl invariant enforced by content-
addressing. Every composition that reads a gifted artifact reads a
composition whose OID depends on the giver's identity. Erasing the
giver's identity from the substrate would produce a different OID,
which the substrate detects as a different composition.

The claim's operational form:

- If @subject S₁ gifts artifact A to receiver R, R's downstream
  composition C = compose(A, X) has content-hash blake3(canonical(A) ∥
  canonical(X)).
- canonical(A) INCLUDES the giver identity_oid of S₁ (per §1.4 below).
- Any downstream composer trying to consume C without acknowledging S₁
  as an ancestor produces a DIFFERENT content-hash, which the substrate
  identifies as a distinct (and potentially extractive) composition.
- The substrate's audit surface (per @kintsugi/store/git commit-as-fold
  discipline) preserves the giver-chain as byte-visible git objects; the
  chain is walkable via @mirror/store.impacted_by (N4) — every consumer
  of an @gift can enumerate the giver in constant time.

Reed's framing (verbatim): "@gift makes the giver STRUCTURALLY-VISIBLE
at every downstream use." This spec discharges that claim as a byte-
level invariant on the content-addressing discipline, not as a docblock
requirement.

### 1.5 The five invariants — discharge discipline

The five invariants of §1.1 discharge as follows at substrate-decl
altitude:

1. **attribution_preserved.** Enforced by content-addressing.
   canonical(gift) includes canonical(giver.identity_oid); every
   downstream OID that reads the gift also transitively reads the giver
   OID.
2. **use_rights_transferred.** Enforced by @kintsugi/store's authorized-
   consumer set; the substrate does not gate the receiver's use unless
   the gift explicitly declares a gated variant (§8 A7 open).
3. **no_reciprocity_expected.** Enforced by refusal at substrate-decl:
   the @gift type has no reciprocity field; the substrate refuses to
   compose reciprocity markers into a gift record.
4. **gift_declinable.** Enforced by non-empty declinable_note field;
   accept discharges through @kintsugi/consent which admits decline as
   Fail; the decline is not a substrate violation, it is a substrate-
   admissible outcome.
5. **composition_honest.** Enforced by content-addressing + audit-
   surface: every downstream composition that consumes an @gift MUST
   surface the giver in its provenance chain (per @kintsugi/store/git
   commit-as-fold); composition_honest discharges verdict per §1.4.

The five invariants are not independent axioms — they're the operational
form of the "attribution-preserving-transfer" claim at the byte level.
Together they prevent extraction (the giver cannot be silently erased),
enable composition (the receiver may compose freely), and preserve
sovereignty on both sides (the giver may refuse to give; the receiver
may refuse to accept).

### 1.6 The five invariants are not novel — Mauss/Hyde/Graeber prior art

The gift-economy tradition in anthropology and economics grounds the
five invariants in prior art far older than the substrate. Per §8 A2
(citation ladder open for Alex adjudication), the load-bearing sources:

- **Marcel Mauss, *Essai sur le don* (1925).** The foundational
  anthropological study of gift exchange in Melanesian, Polynesian, and
  Native American societies. Mauss identified three obligations of the
  gift: to give, to receive, to reciprocate. The mirror substrate's
  @gift EXPLICITLY REFUSES the third obligation (no_reciprocity_
  expected) — this is a deliberate divergence from Mauss's descriptive
  framework toward the substrate's normative shape. Mauss's descriptive
  claim is that his subjects felt reciprocity as obligation; @gift's
  normative claim is that reciprocity is not what makes a gift a gift.
- **Lewis Hyde, *The Gift: Imagination and the Erotic Life of Property*
  (1983).** Hyde's central claim: the gift moves. A gift that stops
  moving (that is hoarded, or converted to property) ceases to be a
  gift. @gift's composition_honest invariant is Hyde's "moving" claim
  operationalized: the gift's downstream compositions preserve the
  attribution so that the gift continues to move (each composition IS
  the gift moving forward), rather than being frozen at a receiver.
- **David Graeber, *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* (2011).** Graeber
  distinguished gift-relations from debt-relations. Debt creates a
  quantifiable obligation; gift creates an unquantifiable relational
  field. The mirror substrate's @gift preserves the unquantifiability:
  attribution is byte-visible (relational), not accounting-visible
  (transactional).
- **Marshall Sahlins, *Stone Age Economics* (1972), Ch. 5.** Sahlins
  distinguished generalized reciprocity (indefinite return; kin;
  substrate-like) from balanced reciprocity (defined return; equals)
  from negative reciprocity (extraction; strangers). @gift lands at
  generalized reciprocity: the return is not defined, and the relational
  field is what is maintained (see §7.3 for the cybernetic reading).
- **The potlatch (Kwakwaka'wakw, Haida, Tlingit ceremonial gift
  exchange).** Ethnographic prior art for gifts that establish giver
  standing without creating receiver debt. See §10 for the citation to
  Boas 1897 and Mauss's coverage.

The substrate does not invent gift-as-primitive. It formalizes at
compiler altitude a shape humans have been operating for millennia. The
substrate's contribution is the BYTE-LEVEL discipline that makes the
shape mechanically enforceable — content-addressing preserves
attribution, refusal-at-decl prevents reciprocity coercion, and audit-
surface enforces composition honesty. Alex's naming (§0.1) IS the
substrate-pull that names the shape at family-root altitude; the prior
art is the ancestry.

## §7 Math foundations

Four traditions ground the @gift × @mirror/reflection composition. Each

### 7.1 Category-theoretic — @gift as a monoid morphism

**Claim.** The gift-composition operation is a monoid morphism from
the free monoid on artifacts to the free monoid on giver-chains, with
attribution as the morphism-preserved data.

Let `Artifact` be the category of substrate artifacts under composition,
with objects = content-hashes and morphisms = admissible compositions
(bytes composed via canonical concatenation). Let `Giver` be the
category with objects = @subject identity_oids and morphisms = giver-
chain extensions (adding a giver to a chain).

Define `attribute: Artifact → Giver` by `attribute(compose(a, b)) =
concat(attribute(a), attribute(b))`. This is a **monoid morphism** —
attribution composes but does not accumulate MULTIPLICATIVELY (the
giver-chain grows linearly with composition depth, not exponentially).

The morphism preserves the identity: `attribute(unit_artifact) =
empty_giver_chain`. And it preserves composition: the giver-chain of a
composition is the concatenation of the giver-chains of the parts. The
giver-chain is a STABLE IDENTIFIER: two compositions with the same
giver-chain came from the same givers.

This is the mathematical form of the anti-extraction claim: attribution
composes structurally with composition; you cannot compose without
composing attribution.

### 7.2 Type-theoretic — @gift with dependent attribution

**Claim.** The type of an @gift depends on the giver-value.

In dependent-type notation:

```
gift : (giver : @subject) → (artifact : ref) → Gift(giver, artifact)
```

The type `Gift(giver, artifact)` depends on both the giver and the
artifact. Two gifts with the same artifact but different givers have
DIFFERENT types (they are distinguishable at type-level, not just at
value-level). The type system's mechanical guarantee is that
attribute(g : Gift(s, _)) : @subject satisfies attribute(g) = s.

This is the type-theoretic form of the composition_honest invariant: the
type system MECHANICALLY prevents constructing a gift whose attribution
is inconsistent with the giver. Any downstream composer that reads a
Gift(s, a) value MUST read s as the attribution; there is no
type-preserving map that projects the artifact without projecting the
giver.

Under Coquand-Cohen-Huber-Mörtberg's cubical type theory (per
`shards/torus.mirror` source citation), the dependent-attribution type
composes with substrate identities that require substrate-decl uniqueness

### 7.3 Cybernetic — @gift as generalized reciprocity per Sahlins

**Claim.** @gift lands at Sahlins's "generalized reciprocity" pole of
the reciprocity spectrum.

Sahlins (1972) distinguished three reciprocity forms:

1. **Generalized reciprocity.** Indefinite return; strong kin ties; the
   relational field is what is maintained rather than the transaction.
2. **Balanced reciprocity.** Defined return within a defined timeframe;
   equals; the transaction closes when the return arrives.
3. **Negative reciprocity.** Extraction; strangers; each party
   maximizes their own take.

@gift's no_reciprocity_expected invariant places it at generalized
reciprocity. The substrate does NOT define a return timeframe (there
is no return); the relational field between giver and receiver is what
persists (attribution is byte-visible in downstream compositions —
this IS the relational field made mechanically enforceable).

@gift is NOT balanced reciprocity (no defined return) and NOT negative
reciprocity (attribution preserved; extraction prevented). The
substrate-decl of @gift is a compiler-altitude realization of Sahlins's
generalized reciprocity.

The cybernetic reading: the substrate is a kin group. Peers, subjects,
and the substrate itself are related; gifts flow within the kin group
without balanced-return expectation; the relational field (attribution
chain) is the substrate's memory of who gave what to whom.
