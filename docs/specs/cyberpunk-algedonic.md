## §2 — What `algedonic` is

### 2.1 Beer 1972/1979's algedonic signal

Beer's algedonic signal (from Greek *algos* = pain, *hedone* =
pleasure) is the **S1→S5 emergency bypass channel** in the Viable
System Model. The normal VSM loop is:

```
S1 (operations) → S2 (coordination) → S3 (audit) ↔ S4 (intelligence)
                                                       ↕
                                                   S5 (policy)
```

The algedonic channel is parallel: when an S1 detects a threat to
system identity that the normal S3-S4-S5 channels would damp out, the
algedonic channel fires *directly to S5*, bypassing S2-S3-S4. Beer
1979 ch. 6 is explicit: the channel is **structural, not
accidental** — a viable system without an algedonic bypass cannot
respond to threats whose normal channels are too slow.

The signal is **typed**, not scalar. Beer's late-life work and the
Reyes/Henao/Hassall 2024 formalisation make this explicit: the
algedonic signal carries a structured payload (consequences,
uncertainties, supporting knowledge, alpha-strength, time-bound) —
not a scalar pain number. The substrate's `Transparency<Ref>` verdict
carrier IS the typed payload Beer was reaching for; cybernetic-cli §6
explicitly identifies the proof block as the structured algedonic
signal in Beer's sense.

### 2.2 Algedonic IS Bateson Level III at the cybernetic altitude

Per the bateson_learning spec §10.2 and the
`architecture-cybernetic-foundation` memory note:

- Bateson Level I = change in response.
- Bateson Level II = change in the *set of responses available*
  (Learning II / deutero-learning).
- Bateson Level III = change in the *system of sets from which
  Learning II draws*. The premises of how-you-learn change.

The algedonic signal IS the substrate's Level III operation at the
cybernetic altitude:

- A normal S3-S4 audit/forecast cycle does **Bateson II**: it
  changes which response the system selects from the standing set of
  responses.
- An algedonic signal does **Bateson III**: it bypasses the standing
  response-set and *changes the premises by which S5 selects responses*.
  The policy layer's *premises* shift, not just its choice.

This is why bateson_learning is the parent species and algedonic is
the Beer-altitude specialization: same operation, Beer altitude vs
Russell-Whitehead altitude. Per recognition #63's parametric framing,
this gives algedonic a **canonical ρ via level-3 restriction**, not
an ad-hoc choice. (See §3.5 for the load-bearing read.)

### 2.4 What altitude does algedonic operate at?

Two altitudes simultaneously, per Read C below:

- **Beer-VSM altitude**: algedonic operates at the S1↔S5 boundary,
  bypassing S2-S3-S4. The "altitude" is the *VSM recursion level* the
  signal crosses.
- **Bateson-Russell-Whitehead altitude**: algedonic operates at the
  Level II → Level III edge of the logical-type hierarchy. The
  "altitude" is the *meta-learning order* the signal lifts to.

These are not two algedonics; they're one operation viewed at two
scales. The substrate already names this in the cybernetic-foundation
work (§6) and cybernetic-cli (the algedonic surface IS both Beer's
bypass AND the operational form of Bateson III).

## §3 — Five reads

Each read tests algedonic against the parametric carrier inherited
from bateson_learning. The verdicts compose; the load-bearing read

### 3.1 Read A — Level-restriction of bateson_learning's graded rep

The simplest read: algedonic is exactly bateson_learning's `ρ_bateson`
graded representation **restricted to the level-3 stratum**. The
type-tuple substitutes:

- `T_reg` = `Premise` (the level-3 RW universe carrier; what the
  system's policy premises ARE)
- `T_regd` = `RegulatedPremise` (the level-2 RW universe carrier
  the level-3 stratum regulates; what S3-S4 select from)
- `τ` = the natural inclusion `V_2 ↪ V_3` paired with the level-3
  projection `V_3 ↠ V_2` — i.e. the standard graded-algebra grading-
  preserving map at level 3 (Kac 1990 ch. 8).

The five cybernetic-ancestor measurements decompose into the level-3
specialisation of bateson_learning's graded-algebra decomposition (§4
below).

**Read A holds:** algedonic IS bateson_learning's graded rep at
level N=3, with type-parameter substitution
`(HierarchyTop, HierarchyBase) ↦ (Premise, RegulatedPremise)`. The
parametric carrier supplies the rep; the species supplies the level
and the type-renaming. No new choice.

The substrate-decl body for Read A alone:

```mirror

use @cyberpunk/coherence<Premise, RegulatedPremise, restrict_grade_3(graded_rep_RW)>
```

Two non-trivial lines. The thinness threshold passes by an order of
magnitude.

### 3.2 Read B — Beer VSM altitude lift

A different reading: algedonic operates at the VSM altitude rather
than the Russell-Whitehead-stratification altitude. The type-tuple is:

- `T_reg` = `S5Policy` (Beer's policy layer; what the system IS)
- `T_regd` = `S1Operation` (Beer's operational layer; what the
  system DOES)
- `τ` = the bypass signal; a non-natural arrow that *skips*
  S2-S3-S4 to land directly at S5

**Read B partially holds.** The structure is recognizable as a
recursion lock — S5 IS a model of the system whose S1 it regulates
(Conant-Ashby applied at the VSM altitude). But the `τ` here is **not
the natural inclusion**; it's the bypass-channel arrow that
deliberately skips intermediate layers. This is structurally a
non-trivial substitution.

Two ways to handle this:

- **B.weakened**: accept that algedonic's `τ` is not natural in the
  Read-A sense. The parametric carrier's "natural substitution"
  language needs to be **read fiberwise** — algedonic's substitution
  is natural *within the algedonic fiber of 𝒢* (the 2-groupoid from
  §11.1 of recursion-locks.md), not across the family-root's
  ambient structure. Per §11.3 of recursion-locks.md: "species-
  substitutions don't compose across species but DO compose within a
  species (same fiber)." Algedonic's bypass arrow IS a 2-arrow in
  algedonic's fiber.
- **B.lifted**: Read B IS Read A at a different altitude. The bypass
  channel IS the natural inclusion at the *Beer altitude*; what's
  not-natural is the *cross-altitude* reading. Read B's "non-natural
  substitution" is Read A's "natural restriction at level 3" viewed
  from VSM coordinates.

Both readings preserve the parametric form. The substrate-pull
recognition is that B.lifted IS Read C: the two altitudes name one
operation, and the operation is parametric-carrier-compatible.

### 3.4 Read D — Form refusal (the unilateral-signal challenge)

A genuine concern: the emergency bypass might NOT split cleanly into
regulator/regulated. An algedonic signal is **unilateral** — it
fires from S1 *to* S5; S5 doesn't fire back during the bypass event.
The Conant-Ashby decomposition assumes the regulator and regulated
are observable parallel-altitude operations; a one-shot bypass signal
is a single arrow, not a parallel pair.

If Read D held, candidate #64 (form refusal at family-root altitude)
would activate. Algedonic would be the species that refuses the
parametric form because the form's binary parallel-pair structure
doesn't fit a unilateral signal.

**Read D does NOT hold.** Three structural reasons:

1. **The signal is bidirectional at the appropriate altitude.** Beer
   1972 ch. 9 + 1979 ch. 6 are explicit: the algedonic signal is
   paired with an S5 *acknowledgment* (the policy layer's adoption of
   the new premise). The bypass channel fires one direction; the
   acknowledgment + premise change fires back. The pair IS the
   regulator (S5 policy adopting new premise) and regulated (S1
   operations whose threat was the trigger).
2. **The Reyes et al. 2024 structured-verdict reading.** A typed
   algedonic signal IS bidirectional by construction: the structured
   payload carries the *gap* between current premise and threat-
   driven premise; S5's acknowledgment IS the closing of that gap.
   This is structurally identical to the property/fracture bilateral
   (recognition #53): a sensing surface + an acting surface.
3. **The substrate's existing implementation.** `reflect.settle` is
   algedonic-triggered; the eigenboard is the regulator; the
   spectrum is the regulated; the trigger condition is the parallel-
   altitude reading. Already operational.

Read D is logged but candidate #64 is NOT activated. Algedonic does
NOT refuse the form; it specifies it differently than Reads A or B
alone, but Read C captures the specification.
