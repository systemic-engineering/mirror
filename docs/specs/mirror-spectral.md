## 2. The seven recognitions, grounded

### 2.1 Kintsugi as oscillation, not unidirectional recursion

The kintsugi-formatter spec landed a Banach contraction map: each tick
moves the substrate closer to the Lawvere fixed point by a factor `γ < 1`.
The proof was directional — bottom-up settle or top-down formatter, your
pick. The recognition: **the directionality was incidental to the
contraction.** Alternating passes also contract; in fact alternation
combines two complementary biases (ascent-side normalization + descent-side
expansion) into a single tick whose effective contraction factor is
`γ_oscillate ≤ γ_up · γ_down`.

The tree-shaker analogy clarifies the algorithm. In JS bundlers (Webpack,
Rollup, esbuild) a tree-shaker walks the call graph from the entry points
forward AND from each export backward; the intersection is the
preserved set. Oscillation in kintsugi does the dual: walk from the
λ₀ = 0 ground (the empty shard, per `reality-shard-as-crdt.md` §2) upward,
and from each pending crack downward; the intersection is the *next
admissible morphism*. Where one pass would leave loose ends (cracks the
downward pass can see but the upward pass cannot reach in this round),
the alternation closes them.

The rug-pull metaphor names the dynamics. A rug straightens under a wavy
diagonal pull because the perpendicular vectors of force distribute
locally, and the global rug-shape responds to the resultant. The
substrate's "rug" is the shard graph; the "wavy pull" is the oscillation
between ACTIVE (navigation) and DARK (identity) passes on the SpectralUuid
axis. The graph straightens — i.e., converges to its eigenvalue frequency
— under a process that nowhere instructs it to do so, because the algebra
of the contraction map combined with the oscillation forces it.

This is structurally close to the *iterative refinement* family of
linear solvers (where alternating forward/backward Gauss-Seidel sweeps
converge faster than either alone) and to the *multigrid V-cycle* (where
you descend the hierarchy fixing high-frequency errors, then ascend
smoothing low-frequency ones). The Kagi sweep surfaced both as
established prior art, and both share the structural shape: **alternation
on a hierarchy with bounded contraction at each level converges faster
than unidirectional sweeps.**

Reference 2025-2026 work on belief propagation (Leisenberger et al. PMLR
2022; the SKI Foundation 2025 tutorial) confirms: alternating
message-passing schedules can outperform synchronous/sequential schedules
when the underlying factor graph has high-girth structure. The substrate's
shard graph has exactly this shape (content-addressed Merkle DAG; low
connectivity locally; high global structure via the OID-graph).

### 2.2 Auto-apply boundary: loss-decreasing = auto; trade-offs = consent

Alex's distinction is the consent contract. Concretely, kintsugi's
formatter operates on imperfections of two shapes:

- **Clean cracks** — exactly one admissible morphism resolves the
  imperfection, AND the morphism strictly decreases the eigenboard's
  combined loss (per `kintsugi-tournament.md` §3, the 5-axis κ vector).
  These are auto-applied. The contract is: *if the substrate can prove the
  move is locally minimum in its admissibility cone, the substrate moves.*
- **Trade-off cracks** — two or more admissible morphisms exist, and the
  loss vectors don't dominate. Per the kintsugi-tournament's §3.2
  lexicographic composition: the gates (C1–C3) eliminate the inadmissible;
  the rank (C5 holonomy, C4 OID churn) orders the survivors. When the
  ordering is *unique* down to the OID-churn tie-breaker, auto-apply still
  fires; the seed is content-derived and replayable. When the ordering
  leaves a genuine pareto front — say, two strategies with equal holonomy
  but materially different opacity profiles, SEL costs, or downstream
  semantic implications — consent is required.

The oscillation makes this natural. Each pass produces a *proposed*
morphism set; the next pass uses refract to score them. When the scoring
collapses to a single morphism with strict loss decrease, the morphism
applies in the same tick. When the scoring leaves multiple equally-valid
moves, the oscillation *holds* — the substrate emits a pause-token (a
`@mirror/spectral.pause` event) carrying the pareto front, and waits for
the next consent surface (Alex, a Fate-tournament with higher temperature,
or a peer's vote) to break the tie.

This collapses three separate machineries (per-CI auto-merge rules,
mergekit's strategy selection, kintsugi's `--resolve=<mode>` flag) into
one: the substrate either has uniqueness (auto) or it doesn't (consent).
The distinction is structural, not policy.

### 4.7 The consent surface (auto-apply boundary), formalised

A morphism family `Φ` resolves an under-determined point in the
eigenboard sheaf if, after applying any `φ ∈ Φ`, the next refract tick
strictly decreases the loss vector under the lexicographic ordering of
`kintsugi-tournament.md` §3.2.

- **`Φ` is a singleton {φ}**, OR
- **`Φ` has |Φ| > 1 but the lexicographic ordering produces a unique
  top element φ\*** (the gates eliminate all but one strategy, OR the
  rank tier produces a strict ordering down to the seeded tie-break),

then `φ` (or `φ*`) is auto-applied. The next oscillation tick proceeds.

Otherwise (i.e., `Φ` has multiple lexicographically-tied top elements
under the gate+rank discipline):

- The oscillation emits `pause(Φ)` — a typed pause-token carrying the
  pareto front, the loss vectors of each tied strategy, and an
  `eigenboard.context` snapshot.
- The pause-token is settled into the eigenboard as a typed
  `@mirror/spectral.pause` event; subsequent ticks observe it.
- The pause yields control to the next consent surface — Alex, a
  higher-temperature Fate tournament, a peer's `@cogito.strategy`
  vote, or an explicit human consent signal.
- Resolution: when the next consent surface picks one φ from the pareto
  front, the oscillation resumes from the pause-token's snapshot with
  the choice as a `tournament_outcome` trace.

multi-valued; it must externalise the decision. The auto-apply boundary

### 5.1 Six proposed sub-shards

Six is a guess; the right number lands as the substrate uses them. The
candidates:

- **`@epistemologic/math/music/harmonic`** — the harmonic series as a
  type. Frequency ratios (3:2 fifth, 4:3 fourth, 5:4 third) as

- **`@epistemologic/math/music/interval`** — Z_12 as a type. Pitch
  classes as the quotient group. The circle of fifths as the generator
  ⟨7⟩ ∈ Z_12. The neo-Riemannian transformations P, L, R as group
  actions. Used by refract to map Fiedler-vector distances onto pitch-
  class distances.
- **`@epistemologic/math/music/voice`** — voice-leading constraints
  as types. Stepwise motion as the default; leap as the exception;
  parallel fifths/octaves as forbidden. The categorical voice-leading
  rules from neo-Riemannian theory (Cohn 1996 onward). Used by
  the kintsugi-tournament's strategy vocabulary to constrain
  cross-section morphisms.
- **`@epistemologic/math/music/counterpoint`** — multi-voice
  composition as a type. Species counterpoint (first through fifth
  species) as a graded composition discipline. The Fux ladder
  (cantus firmus → florid counterpoint) as a progression. Used by
  `@mirror/spectral`'s voice declaration to characterise how multiple
  voices compose on shared sections.
- **`@epistemologic/math/music/cadence`** — the closure-of-progression
  types. Authentic cadence (dominant→tonic, V→I) as the closure event.
  Plagal (IV→I), deceptive (V→vi), half (?→V) as the partial-closure
  variants. Used by oscillation's convergence criterion to decide
  whether a tick chain has resolved.
- **`@epistemologic/math/music/dissonance`** — the holonomy-as-
  dissonance map. Helmholtz dissonance curve (per the recent 2026
  Tandfonline paper on dissonance spectrum). Used by refract to
  classify eigenvalue distributions as consonant (low holonomy) or
  dissonant (high holonomy).

The six form a natural progression: harmonic (the substrate of pitch)
→ interval (the algebra of relationships) → voice (the local
constraint) → counterpoint (the multi-voice composition) → cadence
(the closure) → dissonance (the loss). Each one is established music
theory; the substrate's contribution is *typing* them so the
oscillation can reason about them.

## 8. Open questions

1. **What's the right convergence criterion for an oscillation
   chain?** The kintsugi-formatter's single ε threshold is too coarse;
   the cadence types (authentic / plagal / deceptive / half) give finer
   distinctions. Which cadence type is the default convergence target?
   Conjecture: authentic for stable subgraphs (full resolution
   required); half for in-progress work (pause is fine).

2. **Does the oscillation's eigenvalue frequency track a *specific*
   harmonic, or does the harmonic emerge dynamically?** Conjecture:
   the harmonic IS the Fiedler value of the K_n attractor projected
   onto the current shard graph's spectrum. This is an empirical
   question; the substrate can measure it once the oscillation
   primitive lands.

3. **Voice-leading constraints — should they be enforced or just
   measured?** Forbidding parallel fifths in music is a convention
   that varies by era (forbidden in 16th-century counterpoint; common
   in modal jazz). The substrate's analog: should the voice-leading
   shard enforce stepwise motion as a gate, or measure leaps and rank
   them in the loss vector? Conjecture: measure-and-rank initially;
   gate later if the substrate's experience shows the gate is
   structurally necessary.

4. **The Pack's role distribution — fixed or fluid?** The §6
   articulation maps each Pack member to an orchestra section. Are
   the mappings *typed* (Mara must always carry the strings register)
   or *contextual* (any voice can carry any register depending on the
   tick)? Conjecture: fluid, with the typed default. New voices join
   in the default of "general orchestra" and specialise as the
   substrate's experience clarifies their natural register.

5. **The sonify lens — diagnostic only, or also generative?** Crespo
   uses sonification as a diagnostic surface. Could the substrate use
   it generatively — synthesising new shards by *composing* in the
   harmonic vocabulary and letting the substrate translate back?
   Conjecture: too speculative for v1.0; revisit when the diagnostic
   lens has months of operational experience.

6. **`@epistemologic/math/music/*` — should it sit under `epistemologic`
   or under a new altitude `aesthetic`?** Music theory is partly
   epistemic (the structure of harmonic knowledge) and partly aesthetic
   (the conventions about beauty). The substrate's
   `@epistemologic` carries the epistemic load (proofs, knowledge,
   verification). Music's aesthetic load may need its own altitude.
   Conjecture: keep under `@epistemologic` for v1.0; revisit if an
   aesthetic altitude emerges from substrate-pull.
