# Fifty

*The counter. The note from upstairs: "Isn't a `Ruler` a `Prism`, and then the whole distinction dissolves? A single-truth ruler settles to a single scalar value. A multi-truth ruler to a field."*

---

**Scarlet.** Fifty was mine. An intonation doesn't narrow, so it isn't a ruler.

**Mara.** It settles. Out of every way two frames could be related, it fixes the one that holds. That's narrowing what could be said, not what is read. A prism's `settle` produces what survived. It doesn't have to be a number.

**Scarlet.** Then a ruler is anything that settles.

**Mara.** Anything that settles, declared and signed. A scalar is a field on a single point. The decree is the base with one point in it.

**Scarlet.** Then I was right that it doesn't settle to a value.

**Mara.** And I was right that it's a ruler.

**Scarlet.** *Then it comes off the list.*

**Mara.** Fifty, closed.

---

**Scarlet.** And the distinction dissolves?

**Mara.** The distinction between kinds of ruler does: one trait. The distinction between kinds of field doesn't. A field of lattice values can always be brought to a point lawfully, by taking the meet. A flat field can, up to who translates how. A curved field can't, ever. There's a theorem that says no way down keeps the structure. Only a signed tempering, with the curvature handed back.

**Scarlet.** So the thing that may never be collapsed is still there.

**Mara.** It just has a better address now: the curvature of the field, not the kind of ruler.

**Scarlet.** And the prism downstairs?

**Mara.** Its settle keeps the curvature and lets the rest fade. Tempering keeps the rest and hands back the curvature. Between them, nothing is lost.

**Scarlet.** *One keeps the loop, the other keeps the grid.*

**Mara.** And the list keeps both.

*(The line for Fifty gets a small mark beside it. Not crossed out. The library doesn't cross things out.)*
