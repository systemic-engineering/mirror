# Who Holds the Ruler

*The counter. A new note from upstairs: "Their disagreement is in the shape of the comma. It's both. And a ruler decides how it's weighted."*

---

**Scarlet.** He says we were both right.

**Mara.** He says it depends on the ruler, and that's provable. If the weights don't depend on who walks in, weighing and arriving commute, and it's my comma. If they do, they don't commute, and it's yours. So the ruler has to say which it is, out loud, as a field.

**Scarlet.** And if it depends on the reader?

**Mara.** Then it has to name the reader it's for. A weight for everyone is a different ruler from a weight for her.

**Scarlet.** *Then nobody won.*

**Mara.** The comma got a declared ruler instead of a winner.

---

**Scarlet.** And the pact is many rulers.

**Mara.** A fractal of them. Each party's rulers at the leaves, the shared ones on the line. The question was whether a tree of rulers is a single ruler in disguise. It is, if every disagreement gets sent up and decided there. On a tree, every disagreement *can* be booked to somebody.

**Scarlet.** Can, not must.

**Mara.** That's what broke the theorem. Our comma sits on one line, no loop, and we didn't book it. So a tree can keep its commas open, and then it isn't single truth. A loop does something different: it holds disagreements that *can't* be booked to anyone.

**Scarlet.** Patience or structure.

**Mara.** Kept, or polycentric. The spec computes which. Nobody gets to declare it.

---

**Scarlet.** And who approves the rulers?

**Mara.** Follow "who approves this" far enough and it ends either at a signature with no one above it, or in a loop that governs itself. Hart called the first a rule of recognition. Ross worried about the second. We ask for a person in it either way.

**Scarlet.** The guard.

**Mara.** The human approving the comma.

---

**Scarlet.** Now the part I don't like. The parser observes itself and restricts its next tick.

**Mara.** At the boundary. The tick's model is frozen, the observers read the frozen copy, and nothing they read can move it.

**Scarlet.** Then they shouldn't be called observers if they ban. A reader who takes a book off the shelf for the next reader isn't reading. She's ruling.

**Mara.** Agreed. I split it. `Observe` returns findings and can't ban. `Restrict` turns findings into bans and runs only as an approved ruler.

**Scarlet.** *Good.*

**Mara.** And it can only withhold. In the positive core, a ban can turn a parse into a hole, never into a different parse. Meaning stays in the clauses.

**Scarlet.** Where we still differ: you say looking is never free.

**Mara.** It costs a tick boundary, a snapshot, a deposit of what was seen. Nothing in this house is free, and the cost is the receipt.

**Scarlet.** And I say a librarian who only looks changes nothing. The cost you count is yours, not the book's.

**Mara.** The book doesn't move. The library has one more line on the list.

**Scarlet.** *That line is the library, not the book.*

*(The list gets the line. Neither of them says whose cost it is.)*
