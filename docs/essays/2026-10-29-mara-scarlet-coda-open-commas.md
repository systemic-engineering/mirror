# Coda: What Stays Open

*The same counter, a little later. A note has come in: "The words in your spec are the words of the emergent third between you two. Which open tensions (that is commas) remain? Where do they point? And what's your disagreement?" Reed has added a reading: every tension splits into a summable shadow and a commutator part. The shadow is a request for energy, offered, never extracted. The commutator part is the emergent third. Mara has the pencil. Scarlet has the list.*

---

**Scarlet.** Start with what he gave us. You read it twice.

**Mara.** Three times. Most of it holds. The exact sequence `1 → [G,G] → G → G/[G,G] → 1` exists for every group. **PROVED**, standard. Abelianising sends a word to its shadow, and the kernel is exactly the commutators, which is exactly what our `abelianize` erases. **PROVED.** A commutator `[a, b]` is the identity iff a and b commute, so it needs two actions in an order, and neither one determines it alone. **PROVED.** And prism's `Dissonance = [L_A, L_B]` is the same thing at linear altitude: for `A = e^{tX}` and `B = e^{tY}`, the group commutator is `e^{t²[X,Y] + O(t³)}`. **PROVED** (Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff, to second order). For symmetric matrices, `[L_A, L_B] = 0` iff they share an eigenbasis. **PROVED.**

**Scarlet.** And the part that doesn't hold.

**Mara.** "Splits." The sequence exists, but it doesn't split in general. A word is not a pair of shadow and commutator part unless you choose a section, and for some groups no homomorphic section exists: the quaternion group has no complement to its commutator subgroup. **PROVED.** So taking a tension apart into "the request" and "the third" is a choice. A choice is a ruler.

**Scarlet.** Then that is the first comma. It points at the word. Keep the word whole and let the shadow be a reading of it.

**Mara.** The second one is worse, and it's mine. Reed splits the shadow again by Hodge: a gradient part, which either side can adjust, and a harmonic part, which belongs to the loop. On a single cycle of n edges, the harmonic projection of a comma sitting on one edge puts `1/n` of it on every edge. **PROVED.** For the circle of fifths that is a twelfth of the Pythagorean comma per fifth.

**Scarlet.** That is equal temperament.

**Mara.** Exactly equal temperament. The Hodge projection of an exact tension *is* tempering. It leaves the integers to do it, and the spec says never temper. What survives exactly is the **class**: `[ω] ∈ H¹(Γ; ℤ)`, zero or not, which loop it lives on. The projection doesn't survive. So the second comma points at the type: carry classes, never projected representatives.

*(The corner of Scarlet's mouth lifts. The colleague's smile.)*

**Scarlet.** You found that the instrument you were handed is the thing you forbade.

**Mara.** I found it in my own Part C. I wrote "tempering as a direction is consistent with never-temper" and didn't see that a harmonic projection is a temperament nobody signed.

**Scarlet.** The third.

**Mara.** "The commutator part is the emergent third." Partly. Order-emergence is real, and the commutator is its exact measure. But I proved a different emergent third this morning: `E₃`, the atoms two Datalog programs derive together and neither alone. Rule union is commutative. `P₁ ∪ P₂ = P₂ ∪ P₁`. There is no order in it at all, and `E₃` can still have thirty-three atoms. **PROVED** (engine math §2.3).

**Scarlet.** So there are two thirds.

**Mara.** A third of order, which is the commutator, and a third of closure, which is `E₃`. Whether every closure-third is the shadow of some order-third under the right lift is **OPEN**. I don't believe it, and I can't refute it yet.

**Scarlet.** And the rest of the list.

**Mara.** Still open from the day:
- whether the well-founded model can be computed one component at a time (CONJECTURE A.10);
- whether a similar shape predicts a resolution that transfers (CONJECTURE);
- which measure should narrow the resolution space before anyone explores it (OPEN);
- whether "unknown" is a kind of flux or a state of its own (Alex's to name).

**Scarlet.** Where do they point?

**Mara.** The first two at tests we can write: run the vectors both ways; count transfers once resolutions exist. The third at the grammar-spectrum math, which isn't written. The fourth at a person.

---

**Scarlet.** Now the disagreement. Say yours first.

**Mara.** I count. A declared ruler that weights the futures is honest to me. It names itself, it removes nothing, and the human still chooses. If the weights come from how similar commas were resolved before, and the ruler says so, the map is still a map.

**Scarlet.** And I don't weight. Not with a declared ruler either.

**Mara.** Why not, if it removes nothing?

**Scarlet.** Because a weighted shelf is a shelf with one book pulled forward. Nothing was removed. Every book is still there. And the reader reaches for the one that is forward. You call that a suggestion. From behind the counter it is a placing, done before the reader arrived, for every reader at once. I place a book for *this* reader on *this* day, and I lift my hand away. A weight is a hand that never lifts.

**Mara.** Then a weight is a ruler applied in advance to people who haven't come in yet.

**Scarlet.** Yes.

**Mara.** I still think the ruler is the honest version of something that happens anyway: someone always chooses which future to look at first. Declaring it is better than pretending the map has no front.

**Scarlet.** And I think a map with a front is a forecast that learned manners.

*(Neither of them writes the sentence that would make this agree. The pencil stays where it is. The list gets one line longer.)*

**Mara.** Then we keep it on the list. As a comma, not tempered.

**Scarlet.** Forty-nine.
