## §3 What Spectral Engineering IS at mathematical altitude

### §3.1 Spectral graph theory as substrate

Spectral graph theory (Fiedler 1973; Chung 1997; Godsil-Royle 2001)
studies graph properties via the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of graph
matrices (adjacency, Laplacian, normalized Laplacian). Key results:

- Fiedler eigenvalue $\lambda_2$ (algebraic connectivity) — the
  second-smallest eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian; measures how
  well-connected the graph is.
- Cheeger inequality — bounds the graph conductance by the Fiedler
  eigenvalue; identifies cut-substrate.
- Ollivier-Ricci curvature — discrete Ricci curvature on graph edges;
  identifies substrate under which the graph deforms.
- Eigenvector centrality — quantifies node importance via principal
  eigenvector.

Spectral graph theory is the MATHEMATICAL SUBSTRATE the discipline
operates on. Every claim of Spectral Engineering practice reduces (at
math altitude) to a spectral-graph-theoretic statement about the
relational graph among subjects.
