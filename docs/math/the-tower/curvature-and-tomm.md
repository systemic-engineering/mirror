# Curvature and the Tomm probe

*The curvature 2-form `Ω = dω + ½[ω, ω]` IS the `[D, a]` commutator
IS the Tomm-shaped circular reflexive question. One mathematical
object, three names at three altitudes.*

## §1 The curvature 2-form

For a connection 1-form `ω` on a principal `G`-bundle, the **curvature**
is the `𝔤`-valued 2-form:

```
Ω = dω + ½ [ω, ω]
```

where `[·, ·]` is the Lie bracket of `𝔤` and `d` is exterior derivative.
The curvature measures the failure of the connection to be flat: when
`Ω = 0`, parallel transport around small loops is trivial (no holonomy);
when `Ω ≠ 0`, small loops accumulate non-trivial holonomy proportional
to the enclosed area.

Key identities:

- **Bianchi**: `dΩ + [ω, Ω] = 0` (closed differential form).
- **Ambrose-Singer**: the Lie algebra of `Hol(ω)` is generated by the
  curvature values `Ω(X, Y)` at all points and all tangent vector pairs.
- **Composition**: for two connections `A, B` on a composable bundle,
  `F(A ∘ B) = F(A) + F(B) + [ω(A), ω(B)]` (the cross-term that bundles
  altitudes; see `connections-and-gauge.md` §6).

## §2 The commutator `[D, a]` IS curvature

In a spectral triple `(A, H, D)` derived from a connection `ω` on a
spinor bundle (the Dirac operator of `ω`), the commutator with an
algebra element computes:

```
[D, π(a)] = −i ρ(da)  +  curvature contributions
```

where `ρ` is the representation lifting `da` (the exterior derivative
of `a` viewed as a 0-form) to operators on `H`. Connes (1994) ch. VI
makes this precise: the commutator IS the operator-theoretic realization
of curvature acting on the representation.

Bounded `[D, a]` (the spectral triple axiom) ↔ bounded curvature ↔
bounded holonomy. A spectral triple is a principal bundle whose
connection has bounded curvature, encoded operator-theoretically.

## §4 The reflection-altitude curvature probe

Per `[[architecture-reflection-thinks-in-spectral-questions]]`,
Reflection's thinking IS spectral-altitude selection by Tomm-shaped
commutator probes. The procedure:

1. Pipeline state at tick `n` carries a spectral triple
   `(A_pipeline, H_pipeline, D_pipeline)`.
2. Kintsugi fracture bodies propose candidate morphisms `{M_1, M_2, …}`.
3. For each candidate `M_i` and each Bateson logical type `N`,
   Reflection computes:
   ```
   loss(M_i, N)          = ‖[D_pipeline, M_i] at altitude N‖
   contradictions(M_i, N) = #{ unbounded regions of [D_pipeline, M_i] at N }
   ```
4. Select `(M*, N*) = argmin (α·loss + β·contradictions)`.
5. Tick `n+1` applies `M*` at altitude `N*`.

The `loss` term is the curvature norm; the `contradictions` term is the
count of gap eigenvalues (`λ = 0` or `λ = ∞`) where the commutator
breaks. Reflection's altitude selection IS curvature minimization
over the candidate-by-altitude grid.

## §5 The `[ω, ω]` cross-term IS load-bearing

In the curvature formula `Ω = dω + ½[ω, ω]`, the bracket term is
where altitude transitions live. Without it, the tower collapses
into a flat list of independent altitudes (per the 2026-04-08
connection-substrate design doc): each altitude carries its own loss,
total loss is the sum, no inter-altitude coupling.

With the cross-term, composing altitudes accumulates a curvature
contribution `[ω(altitude_a), ω(altitude_b)]` that depends on the
ORDER of composition (because the bracket is non-commutative). This
is why:

- Reflection's altitude selection matters operationally: `apply M at
  altitude N+1 then descend` is structurally different from `descend
  then apply M at altitude N`.
- The kintsugi loop's order of morphism application matters: applying
  morphism `P` then `Q` versus `Q` then `P` accumulates different
  curvature.
- The librarian's perturbation order matters: moving crystals between
  repos in different orders accumulates different topology curvature.

The substrate's monotone descent `eⁿ⁺¹ ≤ eⁿ` is the statement that
the TOTAL curvature decreases per tick, even though intermediate
steps can increase individual altitude's curvature. This is the
spectral-triple version of energy descent in field theory.

## §6 Gap = unbounded commutator

Where `[D_F, a]` is unbounded, the substrate's frame breaks. These
regions ARE the gaps. The gap shard (per `[[architecture-gap-as-eigenvalue-zero]]`,
Mara fold 2026-06-04) makes this explicit: a gap is a substrate value
defined by the eigenvalue-zero condition on `[D_F, a]`.

Gaps have two flavors:

- **`λ = 0` gaps**: the commutator has a null eigenvector; the frame has
  a flat direction it can't see. Often the kintsugi loop's mutation
  target.
- **`λ = ∞` gaps**: the commutator is unbounded; the frame has a
  direction it sees but can't measure. Often a conundrum (per
  candidate `@mirror/spectral/conundrum`, recognition #247).

Mirror's kintsugi loop closes gaps not by patching (per
`[[feedback-manual-closure-is-training-pull]]`) but by morphism
suggestion — the loop proposes a candidate `M` such that
`[D_F, a]` becomes bounded after applying `M`. The substrate-pull
alignment closes gaps for free; manual patching cannot.

## §7 Connes' bounded-commutator IS the substrate's `transparency<p>`

The substrate carries opacity values `transparency<p>` (per
`shards/mirror/loss/transparency.mirror`). Each `p ∈ [0, 1]` is the
fraction of paths through the substrate where the commutator stays
bounded; `1 − p` is the opacity — the fraction where the commutator
diverges.

`partial(0.97)` means 97% of the paths verified, 3% live in gap
regions. The `opacity_map` carries the located gaps: which sub-paths
have unbounded commutator. This is exactly the data the kintsugi
loop needs to propose morphisms.

So `transparency<p>` is not a verdict in the abstract; it is the
fractional spectral-triple holdup of the bounded-commutator axiom
at the current frame. See `holonomy.md` for the holonomy-family
perspective.
