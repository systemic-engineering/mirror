# Holonomy

*Loss carriers across the substrate are holonomy values — the
structure-group elements accumulated by parallel transport along
composed paths. MirrorLoss, `transparency<p>`, ScalarLoss, the
imperfect verdict family: all members of one mathematical family.*

## §1 Holonomy, formally

For a principal `G`-bundle with connection `ω`, the **holonomy** of
a closed loop `γ: [0, 1] → B` based at `b ∈ B` is the group element
`h(γ) ∈ G` such that parallel transport of any fiber point `e`
around `γ` returns to `e · h(γ)`.

Holonomy is a homomorphism on path concatenation:

```
h(γ₂ * γ₁) = h(γ₂) · h(γ₁)    (left-to-right or right-to-left by convention)
```

The **holonomy group** `Hol(ω, b) ⊆ G` is the subgroup generated by
`{ h(γ) : γ loop at b }`. For path-connected `B`, conjugate choices
of base point give isomorphic holonomy groups.

is the algebraic accumulation, the abelian projection is the

## §3 The composition law

For a sequence of operations `op_1, op_2, …, op_n` on a beam, the
holonomy is the product:

```
h(op_1 ∘ op_2 ∘ ⋯ ∘ op_n) = h(op_1) · h(op_2) · ⋯ · h(op_n)
```

The product is in the structure group, generally non-commutative. The
abelian projection (scalar loss) is the sum:

```
‖h(op_1 ∘ ⋯ ∘ op_n)‖ = norm(h(op_1)) + norm(h(op_2)) + ⋯ + norm(h(op_n))  (only for abelian G; otherwise ≤)
```

For non-abelian `G`, the inequality is strict in general (the
triangle inequality with possibly-strict cross-terms; see Connection
trait `norm()` law in the prism design doc).

This is why `Connection::compose` is non-commutative in general, and
why mirror's `MirrorLoss::combine` appends phases rather than
additively summing scalars: the phases ARE the holonomy values; the
scalar loss is the norm projection that abelianizes them.

## §4 Loop-shaped recursion

A recursive type-level loop in the substrate (e.g., the kintsugi
oscillator's pulse, or the peer's `focus ∘ settle` round-trip) is
a closed loop in the bundle's base space. The holonomy of this loop
is the accumulated structure-group element after one round-trip.

For the trivial-bundle case (flat connection), the loop holonomy is
the identity — every round-trip returns to the same frame. For the
substrate's non-trivial connection (per `curvature-and-tomm.md`),
the loop accumulates non-trivial holonomy — the substrate learns
from round-trips. This is exactly what
`[[architecture-peer-learns-by-crystal-vocabulary-expansion]]`
names: each settle round-trip writes a crystal; the crystal IS the
holonomy of that round-trip.

**The substrate's monotone descent `eⁿ⁺¹ ≤ eⁿ`** is the statement
that the holonomy norm decreases per tick. Per Polyak-Łojasiewicz
with ratio `ρ < 1` (per `[[reference-mirror-spectral-spec]]`),
each loop's holonomy norm contracts by `ρ` toward the identity. The
fixed point of this contraction is `λ₀ = 0` — the trivial-holonomy
ground state where parallel transport is path-independent.

## §5 The verdict family as holonomy components

Mirror's `Imperfect<T, E, L>` verdict has three states: Pass, Partial,
Fail. Each corresponds to a region of the holonomy group:

- **Pass**: holonomy is in a small ball around the identity; the
  bundle's frame returned to the same orientation modulo numerical
  noise. The substrate's verdict surface reads `success`.
- **Partial**: holonomy is non-trivial but bounded; specific sub-paths
  accumulated rotation. The `opacity_map` records which sub-paths.
  The substrate's verdict reads `partial(opacity_map)`.
- **Fail**: holonomy is unbounded (an unbounded `[D, a]` somewhere
  along the path). The substrate's verdict reads `failure(opacity_map)`.

This is why `transparency<p>` carries `p ∈ [0, 1]` rather than
boolean: `p` is the fraction of the holonomy group's volume that
stays in the bounded region; `1 − p` is the unbounded portion. The
verdict surface IS the partitioning of holonomy by region.

## §6 Abelian projection (scalar loss)

For practical computation, the substrate often needs a scalar reading
of holonomy. The norm `‖h‖` projects the structure-group element to
`ℝ≥₀`:

```
norm: G → ℝ≥₀
norm(identity) = 0
norm(g · h) ≤ norm(g) + norm(h)    (sub-additive)
```

This is exactly the `Metric` trait the prism crate's `Transport`
requires (per `prismqueer::bundle`). The substrate's `ScalarLoss`
implements this; `MirrorLoss::norm()` projects to scalar; the
`Imperfect::loss()` reading is this norm.

The sub-additivity is the operational form of the triangle inequality:
for abelian `G`, equality holds; for non-abelian `G`, the cross-term
can inflate the composite norm beyond the sum (per the bundle-tower
framework's non-additive curvature).

## §7 Holonomy of altitude transitions

When the substrate composes operations at adjacent altitudes (e.g.,
a peer pulse followed by a reflection altitude-selection), the
composite holonomy lives in the **semi-direct product**:

```
h_composite ∈ G_peer ⋊ G_reflection
```

The semi-direct structure carries the cross-term `[ω_peer,
ω_reflection]` from the curvature formula. This is why altitude
transitions are load-bearing: the holonomy across altitudes is not
the product of per-altitude holonomies; it's a richer structure that
mirror's `MirrorLoss::combine` is shaped to carry.

## §8 What this enables in the substrate

- The compiler's MirrorLoss IS the bundle holonomy of one compilation
  tick.
- Reflection's score function IS the holonomy norm minimization.
- The kintsugi loop's monotone descent IS contraction of holonomy
  toward identity.
- The librarian's perturbation IS gauge transformation; its quality
  metric is the residual holonomy after perturbation.
- `transparency<p>` IS the partition of holonomy by bounded vs.
  unbounded region.
- Crystal accumulation IS section accumulation; the bundle's
  holonomy structure is preserved under colimit (per
  `[[architecture-mirror-as-expanding-hilbert-space]]`).
