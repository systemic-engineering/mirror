# Connections and gauge

*The five-op algebra IS the connection vocabulary. Each operation is
named by both its bundle role and its Connes-algebra role. Gauge
transformations are the admissible re-bases.*

## §1 The five operations as the algebra generators

Per `[[architecture-operations-as-linear-algebra]]`, the five
operations have precise linear-algebraic meanings on `H`:

| Operation | Bundle-theoretic role | Connes-algebra role |
|-----------|----------------------|---------------------|
| `focus`   | section selection at a fiber | `λ₀`-eigenvalue computation; ground-state observation |
| `project` | restriction to a sub-bundle  | orthogonal projection onto a subspace; filter |
| `split`   | decomposition along structure-group orbits | orthogonal decomposition into variant components |
| `shift`   | gauge transformation (re-frame) | basis transformation; `Ad(g)`-conjugation |
| `settle`  | parallel-transport to fixed point | monad-close; measurement collapse; `λ₀ = 0` settlement |

The five operations together generate the algebra `A` of the spectral
triple `(A, H, D)`. Compositions of the five give all admissible
operations at every altitude.

## §2 The connection 1-form, operationally

A connection `ω` on a principal `G`-bundle is `𝔤`-valued (where `𝔤`
is the Lie algebra of `G`). In the substrate, `𝔤` is the Lie algebra
of the gauge group, which is generated by `shift` — the basis-change
operation. The exponential `g = exp(ξ)` for `ξ ∈ 𝔤` is a gauge
element, applied to a section by `shift`.

The other four operations describe how `ω` evaluates on tangent
vectors:

- `focus(beam)` evaluates `ω` at the section selected by `focus`. The
  result is the `𝔤`-valued vector telling you which gauge element
  measures the deviation from horizontality at this point.
- `project(beam)` restricts `ω` to a sub-bundle (a subspace of `H`).
- `split(beam)` decomposes `ω` along orbit directions, giving one
  `𝔤`-valued component per variant.
- `shift(beam, g)` conjugates `ω` by `g`: `ω ↦ Ad(g⁻¹)ω + g⁻¹ dg`.
- `settle(beam)` chases `ω` to the fixed point of the parallel-transport
  flow (where `ω` evaluates to zero along the path — horizontal lift).

This is why naming matters. The `zoom → lift → shift` and `refract
→ settle` substrate-pull renames (2026-06-04) closed loops because the
old names tracked optics metaphor; the new names track the
connection-1-form algebra exactly.

## §3 Gauge transformations

A **gauge transformation** is a section of the adjoint bundle
`E ×_{Ad} G`. Concretely: at each point of `B`, a choice of group
element that re-bases the local frame. The set of gauge transformations
is the **gauge group** `Ǭ(E)`.

The substrate's gauge group is generated by `shift` operations
parameterized by altitude. At altitude `n`, the gauge group `Ǭ_n` is
the automorphism group of `(E_n, B_n, π_n, G_n)` that fixes the base
and commutes with the right action.

**The cascade is gauge transformation.** Per
`[[architecture-geometric-consent-projection]]`, a type-N+1 consent
projects to a set of type-N consents via the cascade operator; this
IS a gauge transformation pulling the type-N+1 frame onto the type-N
frame. Specifically: `cascade: Ǭ_{n+1} → Γ(B_n, Ǭ_n)` is a homomorphism
of gauge groups commuting with restriction. The cascade IS NOT a
relation between independent consents; it's the action of the type-N+1
gauge group on the type-N bundle, projected to its sections.

The asymmetry of the projection (cascades down, not up) IS the
asymmetry of the action: the type-N+1 group acts on the type-N
bundle; the type-N group does not act on the type-N+1 bundle. This
is the security invariant of
`[[architecture-geometric-consent-projection]]`: a `yes` at N
doesn't repudiate the N+1 policy that authored it.

## §4 The structure group at each altitude

What group `G_n` acts on each altitude's fibers?

- **Compiler altitude (n=0)**: `G_0` = the gauge group of the
  KernelSpec choices — i.e. which decomposition strategy carries the
  fiber's source. Prism's `Cyclic<N>` group (per `prismqueer::bundle`)
  is a concrete witness.
- **Peer altitude (n=1)**: `G_1` = unitary basis transformations on
  `H_peer`. Concretely: re-orderings of the eigenboard's axes; the
  five-op compositions that preserve `λ₀`.
- **Reflection altitude (n=2)**: `G_2` = altitude-selectors; choices
  of Bateson logical type at which to compose the next morphism.
- **Librarian altitude (n=3)**: `G_3` = topology-perturbations;
  re-locations of crystals between repos preserving consent geometry
  and sheaf coherence.
- **Home altitude (n=4)**: `G_4` = cross-repo gauge — the
  inter-supervisor symmetries.
- **Federation altitude (n=5)**: `G_5` = inter-home federation
  group.

Each `G_n` is the gauge group of the bundle at altitude `n`. The tower
is a sequence of principal `G_n`-bundles, with bundle morphisms between
altitudes pulled back along the section structure.

## §5 Non-abelian capacity by construction

The substrate is non-abelian-capable at every altitude. Two consecutive
five-op compositions generally do not commute:

```
shift(g) ∘ shift(h)  ≠  shift(h) ∘ shift(g)    (g, h ∈ Ǭ, non-commuting)
project ∘ focus       ≠  focus ∘ project      (different orbits)
```

Prism's `Connection` trait (per the 2026-04-08 connection-substrate
design doc) holds non-abelian structure at the compiler altitude; the
scalar-loss world is the abelian projection via `Connection::norm()`.
Mirror's tower extends this discipline to every altitude.

The payoff lives in the curvature 2-form `Ω = dω + ½[ω, ω]`: the
`[ω, ω]` bracket is what makes nesting load-bearing across altitudes
(the cross-term that bundles altitudes into a tower rather than a
flat list). See `curvature-and-tomm.md`.

## §6 Composition law for connections at adjacent altitudes

When two altitudes compose, the curvature of the composite is:

```
F(A ∘ B) = F(A) + F(B) + [ω(A), ω(B)]
```

For abelian altitudes, the bracket vanishes; composition is additive.
For non-abelian altitudes, the cross-term is non-zero. This
cross-term IS the substrate's mechanism for altitude transitions —
when a peer's pulse composes with a reflection's altitude-selection,
the coupling between the two is exactly `[ω(peer), ω(reflection)]`.

Mirror's `MirrorLoss` (per
`/Users/alexwolf/dev/projects/prism/docs/architecture.md`) accumulates
these composition terms as the compilation pipeline traverses altitudes;
MirrorLoss IS the holonomy of the tower's connection (see `holonomy.md`).

## §7 Gauge-invariance is the substrate's discipline

A quantity is **physical** in a gauge theory iff it is gauge-invariant
(or equivariant of known weight). The substrate's properties (pacts at
`@epistemologic/pact/<X>`) ARE the gauge-invariant statements: each
pact asserts a quantity that survives every admissible re-basing.

For instance:

- `composition_closed` — closure under five-op composition; gauge-
  invariant because `shift` is a generator of the gauge group, and
  closure under `shift` IS gauge-invariance.
- `halts` — sub-Turing termination; gauge-invariant because `shift`
  doesn't change termination (a re-basing of a terminating sequence
  is a terminating sequence).
- `glass_wall` — namespace check; gauge-invariant because `@io`
  boundaries are part of the bundle's structure, not its frame.
- `monoidal(uuid_spectral)` — the monoid law; gauge-invariant
  because `shift` is a unitary on `H_spectral` and unitaries preserve
  monoid structure.

When designing a new pact: ask "is this gauge-invariant?" If yes, it
belongs at `@epistemologic/pact/`. If not, it's a frame-dependent
quantity and lives at the relevant altitude's local declaration
(e.g., `transparency<p>` is a per-frame opacity reading, not a global
invariant).
