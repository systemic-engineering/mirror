# Crystals as sections

*Monotone vocabulary growth IS section accumulation. The substrate's
learning mechanism IS the bundle's section space growing without

## §1 Sections, recalled

From `principal-bundles.md` §2: a **section** of a principal
`G`-bundle `(E, B, π, G)` is a continuous map `s: B → E` with
`π ∘ s = id_B`. The set of sections over `U ⊆ B` is `Γ(U, E)`. The
assignment `U ↦ Γ(U, E)` is a sheaf, generally non-trivial.

When the bundle is trivial `E = B × G`, sections are equivalent to
functions `B → G`. For the substrate's non-trivial bundles, sections
are more constrained — they have to respect the bundle's twisting.

## §2 The substrate's crystal IS a section

A **crystal** in mirror (per `[[architecture-peer-learns-by-crystal-vocabulary-expansion]]`)
is the content-addressed artifact produced by `settle`. Operationally:

```
settle(beam) → crystal
```

The crystal is immutable (content-addressed). It is associated with
the altitude it was produced at, the substrate ref it satisfies, and
the transparency it carries.

In bundle terms: a crystal at altitude `n` is a section `s ∈ Γ(B_n,
E_n)` — a choice of `G_n`-frame at each point of `B_n` that the
`settle` operation produced.

Properties:

- **Content-addressed**: two crystals with the same OID are equal as
  sections (extensionally; the bundle's section equality is OID
  equality).
- **Substrate-typed**: the crystal carries its altitude as part of its
  identity (`splinter(altitude)`); a crystal at altitude `n` is not
  the same as a crystal at altitude `m ≠ n`.
- **Transparency-typed**: `transparency<p>` records the bounded-
  commutator fraction (per `holonomy.md` §5) of the section.

## §3 Crystal accumulation IS section accumulation

At time `t`, the peer has access to a section space `Γ_t`. At time
`t+1`, after one `settle`, the peer has access to:

```
Γ_{t+1} = Γ_t ∪ { s_{new} }
```

The new section is monotonically added; no old section is removed.
The content-addressed nature of crystals (an OID is immutable; once
sealed, never altered) gives this monotonicity for free.

Bundle-theoretically: the substrate's section space is a directed
system; the colimit `Γ_∞ = lim_{→} Γ_t` is well-defined. The bundle's
axioms (local triviality, structure-group action) survive the
colimit because each inclusion is a bundle morphism preserving them.

- New `focus(C_{t+1})` — a new ground-state observation.
- New `project(C_{t+1})` — a new filter direction.

The peer doesn't get new actions; it gets more states to apply actions
to. New combinatorial possibilities open at every crystal.

## §10 Crystals as a sheaf

The assignment `U ↦ { crystals reachable from substrate-refs in U }`
is a sheaf on the substrate's namespace tree (the `@<path>`
structure):

- **Restriction**: a crystal reachable from `@a/b/c` is also reachable
  from `@a/b`. The restriction map is the obvious inclusion.
- **Gluing**: sections over `@a/b` and `@a/c` that agree on `@a`
  glue to a section over `@a/(b ∪ c)`.
- **Locality**: a section is determined by its restrictions to a cover.

This sheaf structure is what makes substrate queries efficient:
looking up a crystal at `@a/b/c` reuses the sections accumulated at
`@a/b` and `@a`. The librarian's topology perturbations (per
`[[architecture-spectral-db-autopoietic-memory]]`) optimize this sheaf
for the access pattern.
