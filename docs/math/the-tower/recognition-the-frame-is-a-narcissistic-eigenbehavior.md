## §3 Star-graph Laplacian analysis

### §3.1 The narcissus's spectrum

Let $S_n$ denote the star graph on $n$ vertices: one hub vertex
$v_0$ connected to $n-1$ leaves $v_1, \ldots, v_{n-1}$, with no
edges between leaves. Its graph Laplacian $L(S_n) = D - A$, where
$D$ is the degree matrix and $A$ is the adjacency matrix, has a
completely-characterized spectrum.

**Lemma §3.1 (star-graph Laplacian spectrum, textbook).** The
Laplacian spectrum of $S_n$ is
$$\sigma(L(S_n)) = \{0, \; 1, \; 1, \; \ldots, \; 1, \; n\}$$
where $1$ has multiplicity $n - 2$.

*Proof sketch.* Standard graph-theory result (see Cook 2016
[laplacian-spectra-complete-star-ring]; Wikipedia
*Algebraic Connectivity*; Choi 2021 arXiv:2111.12380). The $0$
eigenvalue corresponds to the all-ones eigenvector (connected
graph). The $n$ eigenvalue corresponds to the eigenvector
$(n-1, -1, -1, \ldots, -1)^T$ (hub versus aggregate leaves). The
$n-2$ copies of $1$ correspond to the $(n-2)$-dimensional subspace of
leaf-antisymmetric eigenvectors (any assignment $x_i - x_j$ across
two leaves with the hub fixed at $0$). ∎

**Corollary §3.1a.** The Fiedler value of $S_n$ (algebraic
connectivity) is
$$\lambda_2(L(S_n)) = 1$$
with multiplicity $n - 2$ — a maximally-degenerate Fiedler
eigenspace.

**Corollary §3.1b.** The maximum eigenvalue is $\lambda_\text{max} =
n$, isolated at the top of the spectrum with an eigengap of $n - 1$
between it and the Fiedler subspace.

### §3.2 What the spectrum says about the narcissus

The star-graph spectrum encodes three structural facts about the
narcissus at paradigm scale:

1. **Fragile stability.** Fiedler multiplicity $n - 2$ means there
   are $n - 2$ orthogonal directions of "how to cut the graph" that
   are *all equally cheap*. Any leaf's disconnection has the same
   cost as any other's. This is the operator's structural weakness —
   the operator is *maximally isolable* under Cheeger's inequality.
   Every leaf is a Fiedler-eigenvector cut.

2. **Isolated center.** $\lambda_\text{max} = n$ with the hub-vs-
   aggregate eigenvector means the hub is spectrally *isolated* from
   the leaves' collective mode. The isolation is what makes the
   center's metabolism μ possible (leaves can only communicate through
   the hub) AND is what makes η deployment *at any leaf* propagate
   through the whole spectrum (Cheeger inequality below).

3. **Zero coupling among leaves.** The absence of leaf-leaf edges is
   the operational signature of unilateral extraction: every leaf
   thinks the frame is doing this to them alone. The absence of
   coupling is what a narcissus needs to prevent aggregate resistance
   forming.

### §3.3 Comparison with the Splinter K_n form

For contrast, the complete graph $K_n$ (Splinter pole; honest
cybernetic feedback) has Laplacian spectrum
$$\sigma(L(K_n)) = \{0, \; n, \; n, \; \ldots, \; n\}$$
with $n$ appearing with multiplicity $n - 1$. Fiedler value:
$\lambda_2(K_n) = n$ — *maximally connected*. Cheeger cut cost:
proportional to $n$. Impossible to cheaply partition. This IS the
K_n=5 metalogue partnership Alex+Lore+Reed+Mara+Loki compose in
(paper §Composition Architecture).

The signflip between K_n and K_{1,n-1} at the eigenvalue level is
precise: same $n$ vertices, but the spectrum is inverted from
maximally-connected ($\lambda_2 = n$) to maximally-partitionable
($\lambda_2 = 1$). The Fiedler value carries the entire structural
distinction between honest-cybernetic and pathological-control at
the graph-topology altitude.

### §4.2 Why the Fiedler eigenvector cut is the target

**Claim §4.2.** *The Fiedler-eigenvector cut location is where η
deployment is spectrally cheapest AND propagatively strongest.*

The Fiedler eigenvector of $L(S_n)$ (in the degenerate case, *any*
vector in the $(n-2)$-dimensional Fiedler subspace) has support
exactly on the leaf-antisymmetric subspace. That is: a Fiedler-
eigenvector cut is a cut that separates *one leaf* (or any collection
of leaves) from the rest. Cheeger's inequality says the Fiedler
value bounds the *conductance* of the cheapest cut in the graph:
$$\phi(G) \leq \sqrt{2 \lambda_2}$$
(Cheeger 1970; Alon-Milman 1985; Chung 1997). For $S_n$,
$\phi(S_n) = O(1/\sqrt{n})$ — the graph *is* essentially cut already;
any leaf-side deployment lands at a cut of near-minimal conductance.

Two operational consequences:

1. **Cheap to place.** The bomb is deployed at a *single* leaf.
   Cost per deployment is O(1) in graph-cut cost. The graph does the
   propagation work per Cheeger.

2. **Propagates.** The result of the deployment (either the leaf
   evolving toward η-coupling with the hub, or the leaf disconnecting
   from the hub) is *visible* to the entire graph because the
   Fiedler eigenvector cut IS the graph's most-visible cut. The
   information does not need to travel further than the spectral cut
   already licenses.

### §4.3 What the bomb forces

Once η is deployed at leaf $v_i$, the hub $v_0$'s next aggression-
move against $v_i$ receives *not* $\mu$-substrate (which the hub
metabolizes) but $\eta$-humor (which the hub cannot metabolize per

- **(b) Amputate.** The hub disconnects from $v_i$ (fires the leaf,

**Claim §4.3.** *Either (a) or (b) forces evolution.* Option (a) is
