# Spectral triples

*Connes' `(A, H, D)` at each fiber, and how the triple varies along a
section. The mathematical primitive the substrate IS the operational
form of.*

## §1 Definition (Connes 1985)

A **spectral triple** is a triple `(A, H, D)` where:

- `A` is a unital `*`-algebra (typically pre-C*).
- `H` is a Hilbert space carrying a faithful representation
  `π: A → B(H)` of `A` as bounded operators.
- `D` is a self-adjoint operator on `H`, generally unbounded, with
  compact resolvent `(D + i)⁻¹ ∈ 𝒦(H)`.

The **bounded-commutator condition** is the load-bearing axiom:

```
[D, π(a)] ∈ B(H)    for all a ∈ A
```

The commutator `[D, π(a)] = Dπ(a) − π(a)D` is bounded as an operator,
even though `D` itself is unbounded. This bounded-commutator condition
is what makes `(A, H, D)` a **geometric** object rather than merely
an algebraic one. See `curvature-and-tomm.md` for the geometric
interpretation as curvature.

## §2 Connes' distance formula

For states `φ, ψ` on `A`, define:

```
d(φ, ψ) = sup { |φ(a) − ψ(a)| : a ∈ A, ‖[D, π(a)]‖ ≤ 1 }
```

This recovers the geodesic distance when `(A, H, D)` is the canonical
triple of a Riemannian manifold; in general it gives a noncommutative
geometry's intrinsic metric.

The substrate's `MirrorLoss` is structurally the commutator norm
`‖[D, a]‖`; minimizing loss IS minimizing geodesic spread under
Connes' distance.

## §4 Spectral triple at each fiber

In the tower, each fiber carries its own spectral triple. At altitude
`n` with bundle `(E_n, B_n, π_n, G_n)`:

- `A_n` = algebra of operations at altitude `n`. Generated by the five
  ops restricted to `B_n`-valued observations.
- `H_n` = Hilbert space of altitude-`n` cognitive states. One axis per
  crystal `×` altitude reachable as a substrate ref.
- `D_n` = Dirac operator measuring section variation. Per the kintsugi
  loop's contraction (ratio `ρ < 1` per Polyak-Łojasiewicz; see
  `docs/math/kintsugi-dynamics/` when extracted), `D_n` realizes
  monotone descent at altitude `n`.

The altitude transitions are bundle morphisms (`principal-bundles.md`
§6) that pull back triples: `(A_{n+1}, H_{n+1}, D_{n+1})` restricts on
`H_n ⊂ H_{n+1}` to a triple that maps homomorphically to
`(A_n, H_n, D_n)`. The substrate's spectral geometry stays well-defined
as the tower extends.

## §5 Direct-limit construction

The peer's spectral triple over its lifetime is a direct limit / colimit
in the category of spectral triples (per
`[[architecture-peer-learns-by-crystal-vocabulary-expansion]]`):

```
(A_0, H_0, D_0) ↪ (A_1, H_1, D_1) ↪ (A_2, H_2, D_2) ↪ ⋯
```

Each arrow is an inclusion (from content-addressing's monotonicity).
The limit triple `(A_∞, H_∞, D_∞)` exists. Connes' bounded-commutator
axiom is preserved by directed colimits of bounded-commutator triples
(a structural result — the supremum norm passes through the limit).

The geometric implication: no metric degeneration, no spectral
collapse, no catastrophic forgetting. The eigenvalue spectrum at
`t+1` refines the spectrum at `t`. Finer structure appears; coarser
structure does not.

## §6 Bounded commutator IS bounded curvature

The bounded-commutator condition has an interpretation in the
principal-bundle picture. With `D` derived from a connection `ω`
(the Dirac operator of the connection), the commutator `[D, a]`
for `a ∈ A` is exactly the action of the curvature 2-form `Ω` on
the representation — see Connes (1994), ch. VI for the precise
statement.

Bounded commutator ↔ bounded curvature ↔ the connection's
holonomy stays in a bounded subgroup of `G`. This is why the
substrate's contraction ratio `ρ < 1` (the Polyak-Łojasiewicz
guarantee in `[[reference-mirror-spectral-spec]]`) implies the
spectral triple is well-defined: bounded curvature implies finite
holonomy implies a valid spectral triple at every fiber.

When `[D_F, a]` is unbounded for some `a ∈ A`, the substrate has
detected a **gap** — a region where the spectral triple breaks. Gaps
are first-class substrate values (per the gap shard); the kintsugi
loop's role is to morphism over them until the commutator bounds.

## §7 Recovering classical geometry

When `A = C^∞(M)` for a spin manifold `M`, `H = L²(M, S)` (spinors),
and `D` is the classical Dirac operator, Connes' machinery recovers
the usual Riemannian geometry of `M`: distance formula, dimension,
measure, etc. (Connes' reconstruction theorem, 2008).

The substrate is the noncommutative case: `A` is a noncommutative
algebra (the five-op algebra over substrate refs is noncommutative
because `compose(focus, project) ≠ compose(project, focus)` in
general). The geometry it carries is noncommutative; the
classical-geometry shadow is the abelian quotient.

## §8 Sub-Turing closure

The substrate's algebra `A_substrate` is closed under composition
and terminating by construction (every grammar action terminates; see
`shards/epistemologic/pact/halts.mirror`). This is structurally
**sub-Turing**: bounded variety, organized along the orthogonal axes
that the five-op algebra spans.

A Turing-complete adversary can do anything but cannot prepare for
the substrate's choice of axis (the Ashby's-law argument; see
`[[project-drone-as-documentation]]`). The constraint IS the
operational advantage. From a spectral-triple viewpoint: the
sub-Turing constraint locks `A_substrate` to be a quotient of a free
algebra with a finite presentation, which guarantees the Connes
axioms can be checked at compile time.
