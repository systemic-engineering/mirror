## §1 Clifford algebras Cl(p, q)

### 1.1 Definition

Let `V` be a real vector space with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear
form `B` of signature `(p, q)` (meaning `dim V = p + q`, and B has `p`
positive and `q` negative eigenvalues in an orthogonal basis). The
**Clifford algebra** `Cl(V, B) = Cl(p, q)` is the associative
ℝ-algebra generated by `V` subject to the defining relation:

```
v · v = B(v, v) · 1     for all v ∈ V
```

Equivalently, for `v, w ∈ V`:

```
v · w + w · v = 2 · B(v, w) · 1
```

Or in an orthonormal basis `{e_1, ..., e_{p+q}}` with `η = diag(+^p, -^q)`:

```
{e_μ, e_ν} := e_μ · e_ν + e_ν · e_μ = 2 · η_μν · 1
```

**Universal property**: any linear map `f: V → A` (into an associative
algebra `A`) satisfying `f(v)² = B(v,v)·1_A` extends uniquely to an
algebra map `ḋ: Cl(V, B) → A`.

**Dimension**: `dim_ℝ Cl(p, q) = 2^{p+q}`.

### 1.2 The classification (Atiyah-Bott-Shapiro 1964; Cartan 1908)

Over ℝ, Cl(p, q) has 8-fold periodicity in `p - q` (mod 8):

| p-q mod 8 | Cl(p,q) structure |
|-----------|-------------------|
| 0 | M_{2^{n/2}}(ℝ) |
| 1 | M_{2^{(n-1)/2}}(ℝ) ⊕ M_{2^{(n-1)/2}}(ℝ) |
| 2 | M_{2^{n/2}}(ℝ) |
| 3 | M_{2^{(n-1)/2}}(ℂ) |
| 4 | M_{2^{(n-2)/2}}(ℍ) |
| 5 | M_{2^{(n-3)/2}}(ℍ) ⊕ M_{2^{(n-3)/2}}(ℍ) |
| 6 | M_{2^{(n-2)/2}}(ℍ) |
| 7 | M_{2^{(n-1)/2}}(ℂ) |

where `n = p+q`. Over ℂ, the periodicity collapses to 2-fold:
`Cl_n(ℂ) ≅ M_{2^{n/2}}(ℂ)` for `n` even, `M_{2^{(n-1)/2}}(ℂ) ⊕
M_{2^{(n-1)/2}}(ℂ)` for `n` odd.

This periodicity IS the KO-dimension of Connes' real spectral triple
(per Atiyah 1966 K-theory-and-reality; Connes 1995 §1.1). Mirror's
KO-dim is jointly forward-promised at #101 (γ) + #102 (J).

## §2 Spin groups Spin(N)

### 2.1 Definition

Spin(V, B) is the connected double cover of SO(V, B), sitting inside
Cl(V, B) as the group generated by even products of unit vectors:

```
Spin(V, B) = { v_1 v_2 ⋯ v_{2k} ∈ Cl(V, B) : v_i ∈ V, B(v_i, v_i) = ±1 }
           ∩ { x ∈ Cl(V, B) : α(x) · x = 1 }
```

where `α` is the main involution (identity on `ℝ`, negation on `V`).

**The covering map**: `ρ: Spin(N) → SO(N)` acts by conjugation:
`ρ(x)(v) = x · v · x⁻¹`. The kernel is `{±1} ≅ ℤ/2`. So Spin(N) is
simply connected for `N ≥ 3` while SO(N) has π_1 = ℤ/2.

### 2.2 The double cover matters

A fermion field ψ transforms under Spin(N), not SO(N). Rotating a
fermion by 2π gives `−ψ`, not `ψ`; rotating by 4π returns to `ψ`.
This is what "spin-1/2" means. The Clifford algebra makes this
automatic: multiplying by e_1 · e_2 for orthonormal e_1, e_2 rotates
their plane by π/2 in Cl but by π under conjugation, so the covering
is 2-to-1.

The substrate's #101 (γ chirality) landing at cybernetic altitude uses
`grade` and `flip` actions. Under @spin's lens: `flip` IS conjugation by
the volume element `ω = e_1 · e_2 · ... · e_N`; `flip(flip(c)) = c` IS
the Cl(p,q) axiom `ω² = ±1` (per Atiyah-Bott-Shapiro classification).
Same operator, math reading.

## §3 Spinor bundles

### 3.1 Definition

Let `M` be a Riemannian manifold with tangent bundle `TM`. The frame
bundle `Fr(TM)` is a principal SO(N)-bundle over M. A **spin structure**
on M is a principal Spin(N)-bundle `P_Spin` together with a 2-fold
covering map:

```
π: P_Spin → Fr(TM)
```

such that `π(p · x) = π(p) · ρ(x)` for `p ∈ P_Spin`, `x ∈ Spin(N)`.

Given a spin structure, the **spinor bundle** is the associated vector
bundle:

```
S = P_Spin ×_{Spin(N)} Δ_N
```

where `Δ_N` is the spinor representation of Spin(N) on Cl(N)⊙ℂ (as a
ℤ/2-graded Clifford module).

### 3.2 The obstruction: w₂(M) = 0

Spin structures exist on M iff the **second Stiefel-Whitney class**
`w₂(M) ∈ H²(M; ℤ/2)` vanishes. This is a topological obstruction.

Examples:
- ℝ^n, S^n (n ≥ 2), T^n, Riemann surfaces of any genus: all admit spin
  structures (w₂ = 0).
- ℂP², ℂP⁴, and generally ℂP^{2k}: do NOT admit spin structures
  (w₂ ≠ 0). Would need SpinC (a variant weakening the obstruction).
- The Reck-Clements MZI mesh over U(n): its base manifold is
  U(n) ≅ T^n (topologically the torus); w₂ = 0; spin structures exist
  (potentially multiple; enumerated by H¹(T^n; ℤ/2) = (ℤ/2)^n).

The substrate's `@epistemologic/math/spin/spinor_bundle_representation`
axiom (per §6.6 of the canonical spec) carries `w₂(M) == 0` as an
explicit predicate discharged at the family-of-realization boundary.
When it fails, the substrate falls back to SpinC or fails the spinor
structure existence check outright — depending on the physicality
reading at `@epistemologic/reality/spin/*`.

### 3.3 The Dirac operator

On a spin manifold M with spinor bundle S, the **Dirac operator** is:

```
D_M := γ^μ ∇_μ
```

where `∇` is the spin connection (the lift of the Levi-Civita connection
to the Spin(N) principal bundle) and `γ^μ` are the Clifford generators
acting on sections of S via the spinor representation.

**Load-bearing fact**: `D_M² = ∇^*∇ + R/4` (Lichnerowicz formula), where
`R` is the scalar curvature of M. This connects the Dirac operator to
Riemannian curvature via the Clifford relations. The substrate's D at
each altitude (per `docs/math/the-tower/spectral-triples.md` §4) IS
this Dirac operator when the altitude's H is a spinor bundle.

### 3.4 The Atiyah-Singer index theorem for Dirac

For D_M on a compact spin manifold M of dimension 2n:

```

```

where D_M+ is the chiral Dirac operator (the map from + to - chirality)
and `Â(TM)` is the Â-genus of the tangent bundle. This computes the
kernel-minus-cokernel dimension of D_M+ via a purely topological

## §4 The spin connection and its curvature

### 4.1 The spin connection ω_spin

On the principal Spin(N)-bundle `P_Spin`, the **spin connection**
ω_spin is a 1-form valued in the Lie algebra `spin(N) ≅ so(N)` (both
as Lie algebras; the isomorphism is exponentiated by the covering map).

In a local frame `{e_a}` on M, the spin connection is expressed as:

```
ω_spin = ¼ · ω^{ab}_μ · γ_a · γ_b · dx^μ
```

where `ω^{ab}_μ` are the components of the (usual) Levi-Civita spin
connection and `γ_a · γ_b` (for `a ≠ b`) is the Clifford generator of
rotations in the (a,b) plane.

### 4.2 The spin curvature R_spin

The **spin curvature 2-form** is:

```
R_spin = dω_spin + ½ [ω_spin, ω_spin]
```

By the Clifford relations, this reduces to the Riemannian curvature
`R^{ab}_{μν}` contracted with `γ_a · γ_b`:

```
R_spin = ¼ · R^{ab}_{μν} · γ_a · γ_b · dx^μ ∧ dx^ν
```

Holonomy of ω_spin around loops γ ⊂ M gives elements of Spin(N).
Bounded holonomy ↔ bounded R_spin ↔ bounded [D_M, a] on spinor
bundle ↔ well-defined spectral triple at spin altitude. This chain of
equivalences composes with the substrate's `bounded commutator IS
bounded curvature` from `curvature-and-tomm.md` §2.

### 4.3 The user-frame spin curvature Tomm probe

At user altitude (per `curvature-and-tomm.md` §3's altitude table), the
substrate's Tomm probe at fermion-bearing sites IS the spin curvature
probing the user's frame:


If the commutator is bounded, the user's fermion content is spin-

## §5 The bundle-tower Clifford functor — `spin_lift`

### 5.1 The lift as functor

From the canonical spec §2.3: bundle-tower composition takes altitude N
to altitude N+1 (per `principal-bundles.md` §7). If altitude N carries
Cl(p_N, q_N) and altitude N+1 carries Cl(p_{N+1}, q_{N+1}), the lift is:

```
spin_lift : Cl(p_N, q_N) → Cl(p_{N+1}, q_{N+1})
```

as a homomorphism of algebras respecting the grading and involution.
This IS the Clifford functor on the category of Cl-modules.

### 5.2 The Brain-Mesland-van Suijlekom formalism

Brain-Mesland-van Suijlekom 2013 (arXiv:1306.1951, "Gauge Theory for
Spectral Triples and the Unbounded Kasparov Product") gives the
unbounded-KK-theoretic formulation of Cl-functorial composition of
spectral triples. Given spectral triples `T_1 = (A_1, H_1, D_1)` at
altitude N and `T_2 = (A_2, H_2, D_2)` at altitude N+1, their
unbounded Kasparov product `T_1 #_A T_2` IS the composed spectral
triple at altitude N+1.

Substrate reading:

- `#100` (@spectral/metalogue) is the Mesland-category framing at
  substrate-altitude; @spin's `spin_lift` IS the Cl-typed refinement of
  @spectral/metalogue's morphisms.
- The Mesland category admits `γ` and `J` as morphism-preserving data
  (per Dabrowski-D'Andrea-Magee 2019 "Twisted reality and the second-

### 5.3 Composition is (in general) non-commutative

Just as bundle-tower composition is non-commutative for non-abelian G
(per `principal-bundles.md` §5), Clifford functor composition is
non-commutative when the Cl algebras themselves are non-commutative.
The non-commutativity is measured by the **spin curvature at the
composition boundary** — the cross-term `[ω_spin(altitude_N),
ω_spin(altitude_{N+1})]`.

This matters operationally: applying morphisms at multiple altitudes
in different orders accumulates different total spin curvature. The
substrate's monotone descent `e^{n+1} ≤ e^n` holds for total curvature
integrated over the tower; intermediate altitudes can increase
spin-curvature-at-that-altitude. Same shape as the general
curvature-and-tomm case (§5 of that doc), specialized to spin altitude.

## §6 The physical instantiations

### 6.1 Cl(1, 3) at Lorentz altitude — relativistic fermions

The Dirac gammas satisfy `{γ^μ, γ^ν} = 2η^μν · 1` in Lorentz signature
(+ - - -) or (- + + +) (both conventions in use). Cl(1, 3) ≅ M_2(ℍ) as
real algebra, or M_4(ℂ) after complexification. Spin(1, 3) = SL(2, ℂ)
(as a real 6-dimensional Lie group).

The chirality operator: `γ^5 := iγ^0γ^1γ^2γ^3`; satisfies `(γ^5)² = 1`
and anti-commutes with all γ^μ. It IS the Cl(1,3) volume element up to
normalization. This is what #101's `flip` action IS at Lorentz altitude:
left-multiplication by γ^5.

The charge conjugation operator: `C = iγ^2γ^0` (in the Dirac basis).
Satisfies `Cγ^μC⁻¹ = -(γ^μ)^T`. The full J at Connes altitude is
`J = C · K` where K is complex conjugation — anti-linear.

### 6.2 Cl(0, 3) at Pauli altitude — silicon qubit rotations

signature. Cl(0, 3) ≅ ℍ ⊕ ℍ (two copies of quaternions; even and odd
parts split). Spin(3) = SU(2).
