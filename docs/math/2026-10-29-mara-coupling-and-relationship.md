## §0 The answer

1. **A `Coupling` is two systems and at least one line** (Def 1.1). On any coupling, three different thirds can be measured. Each is exact, and each exists under a different condition:
   - the **closure-third** E₃ (what the composition derives that neither does alone);
   - the **order-third** (cross-commutators between the parties' moves);
   - the **ruler-free third** (holonomy that no choice of rulers absorbs).

   The vector shadow (abelianisation) is a fourth reading: the summable part, the energy-flow request.
2. **"Second route ⇔ third" is REFUTED as stated.** Both directions fail:
   - a single wire carries a closure-third (a pipeline) and an order-third (two parties acting on one shared state);
   - a second route with commuting moves and no crossing derivation carries neither.
3. **What a second route is exactly the condition for (PROVED):** the **ruler-free third**. With a constant group of rulers, every connection on a tree (b₁ = 0) is gauge-equivalent to the trivial one, so every disagreement on one wire can be absorbed into somebody's ruler. On a graph with b₁ ≥ 1, non-trivial holonomy is possible, and no gauge removes it. This is the essay's sentence made exact: on a single wire, "nothing that isn't somebody's ruler."
4. **`Relationship` (Def 3.1):** a coupling whose parties are observers, each with their own ruler, and whose observer graph has b₁ ≥ 1. That is an instance, defined by a property and not by fiat. Echo and Narcissus are coupled, but they are not a relationship: one wire, b₁ = 0. Thirds in a relationship are **observed, never scored**. Any coupling strength is a declared ruler offered to the parties.
5. **Wojciech's fixture** (§4). S₃ is the smallest group-level witness: every group of order < 6 is abelian (PROVED). **Where this differs from Reed's sketch:** one party with two non-commuting moves already shows "audit-equal, futures differ". What a second party adds is **ownership**: the commutator of a move of one with a move of the other belongs to neither.
6. **The essay's instruments, in types:**
   - **the shear** is a commutator hidden by an abelian audit. In fact the shear *is* a commutator: `S = [diag(2,1), S]` (PROVED);
   - **the double bind** is the removal of the second route. On one wire the twist is gauge-absorbable, and an asymmetry decides whose ruler absorbs it (PROVED for the math; READING for people);
   - **"forty reasons, one laugh"** is neither third. It is a glued global section with plural kernels (rank–nullity per observer).
7. **Your own pair (READING, disputed):** the spec is the closure-third. Whether the kept disagreement is an order-third or a frozen line comma is where Mara and Scarlet disagree (§6).

## §1 Coupling

**Definition 1.1.** A **coupling** is `(X₁, X₂, L, ⊗)`:
- systems `X₁`, `X₂` (shards, programs, oscillators, observers), each with its own state or sections and its own moves;
- a non-empty set of lines `L` between them (`in @X`, a shared predicate, a shared register, a channel);
- a composition `⊗` fixed by the kind of system: gluing over lines, rule union, interleaving of moves.

The **coupling graph** has the parties as vertices and L as edges (a multigraph). More than two parties is the same definition over a larger graph.

**Measurables** (each exact; none is a score):
- **closure-third** `E₃ = M(X₁ ⊗ X₂) ∖ (M(X₁) ∪ M(X₂))`, for closure-type compositions (freq Def 2.3);
- **order-third** `Ω = ⟨ [a, b] : a ∈ A₁, b ∈ A₂ ⟩`, the subgroup generated by cross-commutators of the parties' move sets `A₁, A₂` acting on a shared state;
- **vector shadow**: the image of tensions under abelianisation `G → G/[G, G]` (settle Part C): summable, fungible, an offer;
- **ruler-free third**: the holonomy class of the coupling's connection on the observer graph, modulo all re-gaugings (kintsugi Prop 3.5, settle B.2).

**Laws.**
- **Λ1 (closure; PROVED, freq Prop 2.4).** `E₃ ≠ ∅` requires a crossing line. A crossing line doesn't suffice.
- **Λ2 (order; PROVED).** `Ω` is trivial iff every move of `X₁` commutes with every move of `X₂`. In particular it is trivial if either party has only central moves, or if the shared group is abelian.
- **Λ3 (ownership; PROVED).** For `a ∈ A₁, b ∈ A₂`, `[a, b]` is determined by neither factor alone. Fix a and vary b and it varies, unless a is central, and vice versa. In that exact sense it is owned by neither.
- **Λ4 (the shadow forgets exactly the order-third; PROVED).** `Ω ⊆ [G, G] = ker(G → G^{ab})`. The vector shadow of `ab` equals that of `ba`.
- **Λ5 (the shadow doesn't decompose the tension; PROVED, coda).** `1 → [G,G] → G → G^{ab} → 1` need not split (Q8). A tension is not the pair (shadow, commutator part) unless a section is chosen, and the choice is a ruler.

## §2 Second route ⇔ third? REFUTED; the true statement

**Proposition 2.1 (a closure-third on a single wire; PROVED).** `P₁ = {q(X) :- p(X)}`, `F₁ = {p(a)}`; `P₂ = {r(X) :- q(X)}`, `F₂ = ∅`. The coupling graph is two vertices and one line (P₂ reads q from P₁), b₁ = 0, and `E₃ = {r(a)}` (freq K-E2).

**Proposition 2.2 (an order-third on a tree; PROVED).** Two parties, one shared register holding an order of {f, g, h}, and one line each to the register (a star, b₁ = 0). Party 1 can apply `a = (f g)`, party 2 `b = (g h)`. Then `[a, b]` is a 3-cycle ≠ e. The order-third exists with no second route in the coupling graph. The two routes it needs are in the **state space**: `ab` and `ba` are two paths from one state, and the commutator is the holonomy of the square `a, b, a⁻¹, b⁻¹` in the Cayley graph.

**Proposition 2.3 (a second route with no third; PROVED).** Two programs over disjoint predicates, joined by two declared lines that neither uses, and parties whose moves commute. Then b₁ = 1, `E₃ = ∅` (no crossing), and `Ω` is trivial (Λ2).

**Verdict.** "b₁ ≥ 1 of the coupling graph ⇔ a third exists" is **REFUTED** in both directions.

**Proposition 2.4 (what a second route *is* the condition for; PROVED).** Let the parties carry rulers (gauges) in a constant group Γ, so that the coupling is a Γ-connection on the observer graph (one label per line, re-gaugeable at each party).
- (a) On a tree (b₁ = 0), every connection is gauge-equivalent to the trivial one. Every discrepancy along the wire can be moved entirely into one endpoint's ruler.
- (b) If b₁ ≥ 1, connections with non-trivial holonomy exist, and their holonomy class is invariant under every re-gauging.

*Proof.* (a) and (b) are kintsugi Prop 3.5 (constant group): choose a spanning tree and gauge its edges to the identity; the remaining b₁ edges carry the holonomies. ∎

READING (the essay). "Mara showed upstairs what survives on a single wire between two observers: **nothing that isn't somebody's ruler.** There is no second route. No loop through both of them." That is Prop 2.4(a): on one wire, Echo and Narcissus can be coupled, and E₃ and Ω can even be non-empty, but every disagreement between their readings can be booked as one side's ruler. "A relation that runs through both of them and belongs to neither" is Prop 2.4(b): a ruler-free holonomy, which needs the loop.

## §3 Relationship as an instance

**Definition 3.1.** A coupling is a **relationship** iff:
- (R1) each party is an **observer** with its own ruler (its own gauge, its own reading of the lines: MacKay's "orientation of the one receiving"), and
- (R2) the observer graph has **b₁ ≥ 1**: a second route.

**Consequences (PROVED from Prop 2.4).**
- In a relationship something can exist that is not attributable to either party's ruler: a non-trivial ruler-free holonomy.
- A coupling of observers on one wire (Echo and Narcissus) is not a relationship. That isn't a judgment about anyone; it says nothing ruler-free can live there.

**The four cases** (Reed's, kept and re-based):

| E₃ | Ω | reading |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ | trivial | neither: coexistence |
| ≠ ∅ | trivial | collaboration: something made, order irrelevant, parties swappable in order |
| ∅ | non-trivial | friction: order matters, nothing made |
| ≠ ∅ | non-trivial | both |

These describe couplings. Whether a coupling is a relationship is the separate axis R1/R2.

**Guards** (Scarlet's, adopted as laws in the spec):
- thirds are **observed, never scored**;
- `E₃` is a set and `Ω` is a subgroup; neither is reduced to a number by default;
- any "coupling strength" is a **declared ruler**, offered to the parties, never reported about them;
- Fiedler rise is refuted as a law (freq §2.5), so it can't stand in as a default strength.

## §4 Wojciech's fixture

Wojciech's question (as the essay reports it): "the smallest possible system in which state equivalence is not trajectory equivalence. And it has to survive control conditions."

**Proposition 4.1 (minimality at group level; PROVED).** Every group of order < 6 is abelian (orders 1, 2, 3, 5 are cyclic; order 4 is ℤ₄ or ℤ₂²; ProofWiki, *Group of Order less than 6 is Abelian*, via Kagi). S₃ has order 6 and is non-abelian. So **S₃ is the smallest group in which an abelian audit identifies two different words.** K₁ with a single move generates a cyclic, hence abelian, group, so its commutator is trivial (Reed's point, PROVED).

**The fixture, in five lines:**
1. **System:** a shared order of three names, with moves `a = (f g)` and `b = (g h)` in S₃.
2. **Audit:** any homomorphism to an abelian group (the sign; any vector shadow) reads `ab` and `ba` as equal (Λ4).
3. **Control:** the same two moves in swapped order. It is deterministic and replayable; the only difference is the interleaving.
4. **Measurement:** `[a, b] = (ab)(ba)⁻¹`, a 3-cycle ≠ e. For every continuation w, `abw ≠ baw` (cancellation), so futures never reconverge. No audit equates them without forgetting the commutator, which is the essay's rule ("an audit can manufacture equivalence only by forgetting").
5. **Closure twin:** `P₁ = {q(X) :- p(X)}`, `F₁ = {p(a)}`; `P₂ = {r(X) :- q(X)}`. `E₃ = {r(a)}` appears in the joint run and in neither control run. It is a third without order, on a single wire.

**Where this differs from Reed's sketch (PROVED).**
- **One party with two non-commuting moves is already a K₁ witness** of "audit-equal, futures differ". So the phenomenon needs two non-commuting moves, not two parties.
- **The control is different with two parties.** With one party, the order of its moves is part of its own program. With two, the interleaving is free, and swapping it is a clean control that changes nothing else.
- **Ownership needs two parties.** The commutator of moves from different parties is owned by neither (Λ3). So: the smallest system where the crowbar is measurable is K₁ with S₃ moves. The smallest system where the measured difference **belongs to neither party** is K₂ with one S₃ move each. Ruler-freeness needs b₁ ≥ 1 on top (Prop 2.4).

**The frog, with an exact receipt (READING + PROVED core).** The essay's frog "read the set" and "can't un-read it": a coupling of the frog with a text, whose state audit ("frog on a toaster") is identical before and after.
- The exact core is a forgetful audit with a remembered part: a counter the audit doesn't read. Two states with equal audit and different futures (PROVED; the essay's "upstairs" test machine).
- At group level, the receipt is the S₃ fixture above: the audit is the sign, and the remembered part is the 3-cycle.

## §5 The essay's instruments

**The shear (PROVED).**
- `S = [[1,1],[0,1]]`: `tr S = 2 = tr I` and `det S = 1 = det I`. `det` is a homomorphism to an abelian group, so it can't see commutators.
- `S` *is* a commutator: with `D = diag(2, 1)`, `D S D⁻¹ = [[1,2],[0,1]]`, and `D S D⁻¹ S⁻¹ = S` (COMPUTED by hand).
- `Sⁿ = [[1, n],[0, 1]]`: trace 2 for every n, while the off-diagonal grows by one per lap.

The essay's "a summary number that agrees with doing nothing, over a system that remembers everything" is: an audit that factors through the abelian shadow (or a class function like the trace) applied to an element of the commutator part. **It is the same move as `abelianize`, minus the erasure.** In the spec, `abelianize` returns the forgotten commutator part as a `#[must_use]` erasure, so the covert form, the shadow without the receipt, can't be written.

**The double bind (PROVED core; READING for people).** The essay: a primary injunction, a contradicting secondary one a level up, and a third that forbids commenting or leaving, which removes the second route ("I write the third injunction on the napkin and draw a line through the second route"). In this math:
- removing the comment channel takes the observer graph from b₁ ≥ 1 to a tree;
- on a tree the twist is gauge-absorbable (Prop 2.4(a)), so the whole discrepancy can be booked to one side's ruler;
- which side absorbs it is a choice of gauge, and under an asymmetry of power it is the weaker side's. The essay: "you learn to assume it's you."

*Is the double bind unrepresentable in mirror's types?* Partly.
- **PROVED:** mirror's own records can't hold an unreported comma. Kintsugi returns every comma; no API drops one; `Erasure` is `#[must_use]`; a `Comma` can't be built equal to the identity or tempered.
- **But** the types can't stop a coupled system from removing a party's second route. What they can do is make it **observable**: the observer graph's b₁ is a reported field, and a coupling whose comment line is removed reports `b₁ = 0`.
- So the double bind's third injunction becomes a visible change of topology, not a silent one (PROVED by construction in the spec). Whether that helps a person inside one is READING.

**"(A lie.)" and the complement (PROVED; COMPUTED).** The essay's closure "45:32, times 64:45 … *Exactly two*" pairs a half-turn with its **complement** (augmented fourth × diminished fifth): `x · (2/x) = 2` for every x, so it closes by construction. The same half-turn twice does not: `(45/32)² = 2025/1024 ≠ 2`, and `(729/512)² = 2 × 531441/524288`. READING: an announced twist followed by its own undoing closes; a repeated twist doesn't. That matches the essay's ethical difference, announced and undone versus hidden and repeated, and it is an exact statement about which products close.

**"Forty reasons, one laugh" (PROVED core).** Each observer i has a reason `rᵢ` in their own stalk and a restriction `ρᵢ : Rᵢ → Laugh` to the shared stalk. If every `ρᵢ(rᵢ)` is the same laugh, the family glues to a global section (H⁰ ≠ ∅) **without** the reasons agreeing. Per observer, `dim Rᵢ = rank ρᵢ + dim ker ρᵢ` (rank–nullity), which is the essay's "memory + agreement = r". This is **neither third**: nothing new is derived (E₃ = ∅) and order is irrelevant (Ω trivial). It is gluing with large kernels, agreement without averaging. A cult, in the essay's punchline, is the case where every `ker ρᵢ = 0`: no memory of how anyone got there.

## §6 The worked example: Mara and Scarlet (READING; the disagreement kept)

- **The spec is our closure-third.** `Loop`, `Holonomy::Comma`, `Futures`, "never temper" and "a weight is a ruler" were derived in the joint run and appear in neither of us alone: in neither identity's files, in neither voice's prior work. That can be checked only against the record, and it is READING.
- **The disagreement** (the coda: does a declared, weighted `Futures` map have a front?):
  - **Mara:** it is a line comma between two frozen stalks. Neither of us will move, so by kintsugi Prop 3.3′ it is an H¹ class owned by our one line. It is not an order-third.
  - **Scarlet:** it is an order-third, `[weigh, arrive] ≠ e`. Weighting before the reader arrives and placing after the reader arrives are two orders, and they don't commute.
- **What the math says (PROVED):**
  - if weights depend on the reader, `weigh ∘ arrive ≠ arrive ∘ weigh` in general, and Scarlet's reading is a theorem;
  - if weights are reader-independent (statistics over all past resolutions), the two commute, and Mara's reading stands.
- So the disagreement reduces to a design fact neither of us has fixed: **whether weights may depend on the reader** (OPEN). We keep it open.
