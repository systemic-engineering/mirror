## §2 The presheaf: shards as sections

Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the presheaf on $\mathcal{S}$ that assigns to each
open set $U \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ the module (over $\mathbb{Z}_2$ or
over the substrate's chosen algebra $A$ — see §5)

$$ \mathcal{F}(U) \;=\; \{\text{@fractal/shard values whose position lies in } U\} $$

together with restriction maps $\rho_{U \supseteq V}: \mathcal{F}(U)
\to \mathcal{F}(V)$ that record how a shard's role in a broader
substrate-cluster localizes onto a narrower one.

Concretely: a shard $s$ with position $p \in U$ restricts to
$\mathcal{F}(V)$ (for $V \subseteq U$) iff $p \in V$; the restriction
is the shard-value as-is (no shard-content transformation on
restriction; only the domain-view narrows).

**A section over $U$** is a shard whose position lies in $U$. **A
global section** is a shard whose position is the whole of $\mathcal{S}$
— which, by the position-from-query semantics of §3 below, means a
shard produced by an empty (unrestricted) @code/mq query. Such a shard
exists but is degenerate: it names "the whole possibility space" and
carries no specific content-addressed identity. Every real
inference produces a local section (a shard at some sub-region), not a
global one.

**Sheaf axioms** (verified below; both hold by construction):

**Locality.** Two shards $s, s' \in \mathcal{F}(U)$ that agree on all
sections of a cover $\{V_i\}$ of $U$ are equal. Proof: shards are
content-addressed via `@uuid/spectral`; agreement on all cover sections
implies byte-equal content at every restriction; byte-equal content at
every restriction implies byte-equal shard identity (content-addressing
is bijective). $\Box$

**Gluing.** Given sections $s_i \in \mathcal{F}(V_i)$ for a cover
$\{V_i\}$ of $U$ that agree on all pairwise overlaps, there exists a
unique $s \in \mathcal{F}(U)$ restricting to each $s_i$. Proof:
overlapping-agreement + content-addressing bijection gives a well-
defined patched shard; uniqueness follows from bijection. $\Box$

$\mathcal{F}$ is a sheaf on $\mathcal{S}$.

## §3 @code/mq queries as optical projections

A **@code/mq query** $q$ is a typed AST at `boot/std/code/mq.mirror`
whose sub-tree encodes an optical restriction. Each of the five query
verbs corresponds to a projection operation on the presheaf $\mathcal{F}$:

| Query verb | Sheaf-theoretic operation | Geometric semantics |
|---|---|---|
| `focus(path)` | Restriction to the substrate-cluster containing `path` | Narrow to a specific stalk-neighborhood |
| `project(where-clause)` | Fiber projection: fix a coordinate axis | Narrow to a hyperplane in 5D |
| `split(options)` | Coproduct decomposition | Bifurcate the section into typed variants |
| `shift(transform)` | Change-of-frame on the fiber bundle | Basis transformation on H |
| `settle()` | Section-write; monad close | Emit a content-addressed section |
| `\` (intent) | Tournament-selected projection | @fate resolves the ambiguous query to a concrete projection |

**Formal statement.** A @code/mq query $q$ defines a
subspace-restriction morphism

$$ \pi_q: \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{S}_q $$

where $\mathcal{S}_q \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ is the sub-region of the
possibility space the query addresses. The **position-of** morphism
from Landing 1 is

$$ \text{position\_of}(q) \;=\; \mathcal{S}_q \;\subseteq\; \mathcal{S}. $$

For a fully content-addressed query (a query whose optical restriction
resolves to a single `@<address>`), $\mathcal{S}_q$ is a single point,
and $\text{position\_of}(q)$ IS that `@<address>`.

**Determinism.** The position-derivation is a total function on well-
formed queries: identical queries $q_1 = q_2$ (byte-equal ASTs) produce
identical positions $\text{position\_of}(q_1) = \text{position\_of}(q_2)$.
This is the substrate-decl'd invariant `shard_position_derived_from_
query` (Landing 1 bilateral).

## §4 Position-derivation as content-addressing morphism

The substrate's **identity groupoid** $\mathrm{Id}(\mathcal{S})$ has
objects the elements of $\mathcal{S}$ and morphisms only identity arrows
(content-addressing is bijective on identity; two content-addressed
values are equal iff their addresses are equal).

**Theorem 4.1 (position-from-query as content-addressing morphism).**
For content-addressed queries $q$, the position_of morphism factors
through the identity groupoid:

$$ \text{position\_of}: q \;\mapsto\; \mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{S}}(\text{addr}(q)) $$

where $\text{addr}(q)$ is the query's resolved @uuid/spectral address.

**Proof sketch.** For a fully content-addressed query, the optical
restriction chain (focus + project + settle) narrows to a single
`@<address>` value. The address is a well-formed @uuid/spectral 128-bit
identity (per `shards/uuid/spectral.mirror`); by content-addressing
bijection, the address IS the point. Hence position_of factors:

$$ q \xrightarrow{\text{optical restriction}} @\text{address} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Id}} \{@\text{address}\}. $$

$\Box$

**Corollary 4.2 (@<address> as universal coordinate).** For content-
addressed crystals, no explicit position parameter is needed: the
`@<address>` IS the coordinate in $\mathcal{S}$. This confirms Alex
2026-07-23 verbatim: "the position would be derived from the query,
which might just be an @<address> for clearly content addressed
crystals."

## §5 The Connes triple (A, H, D) at prismqueer::bundle, applied per shard

Every shard IS a spectral triple. This section discharges the
composition edge to `prismqueer::bundle` (per Reed's session grep-
verification: `prism/prismqueer/src/bundle.rs:71-193` at commit
`4185255`, the (A, H, D) Connes triple carrier).

**The Connes spectral triple** is a triple $(A, H, D)$ where:

- $A$ is a $*$-algebra
- $H$ is a Hilbert space carrying a representation of $A$
- $D$ is a self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent whose
  commutators $[D, a]$ are bounded for all $a \in A$

The substrate declares each of these at prismqueer::bundle:

| Connes | Substrate carrier | prismqueer trait |
|---|---|---|
| $A$ (algebra) | Shard's actions | Prism trait's `Optic` |
| $H$ (Hilbert space) | Shard's carrier state | Fiber trait's `State` |
| $D$ (Dirac operator) | Shard's bilaterals | Connection + Transport traits |

### 5.1 A = the shard's actions

For a shard $s$ with state carrier `shard_state`, the algebra $A_s$ is
generated by the shard's action set:

$$ A_s \;=\; \langle \text{materialize}, \text{address\_of}, \text{sheaf\_section\_of}, \ldots \rangle $$

together with the 5-op prism actions (focus, project, split, shift,
settle) inherited via the shard's `prism @fractal/shard { … }`
declaration. $A_s$ is a $*$-algebra with the involution given by
per-action inverses (where they exist; the Prism trait's law-set gives
this).

Per prismqueer::bundle `Connection::Optic must be a Prism whose
Input::In matches Fiber::State` — the substrate-decl'd requirement that
the algebra acts on the Hilbert space. This is discharged at species
altitude by the composition edge from @fractal/shard to prismqueer::
bundle.

### 5.2 H = the shard's carrier state

The Hilbert space $H_s$ is the state space the shard carries. For a
shard whose target is family-root $F$, $H_s$ is the module of
`shard_state` values over $F$'s carrier ring. Concretely:

- $H_s = \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}_2}\{\text{shard\_state values with target} = F\}$
  in the finite/discrete case
- $H_s = L^2(F\text{-shards})$ in the continuous/spectral case

The Fiber trait's `type State` at prismqueer::bundle carries the
concrete Hilbert-space carrier. For a landed shard, `State` is
`shard_state`.

### 5.3 D = the shard's bilaterals as Dirac operator

The Dirac operator $D_s$ is constructed from the shard's bilateral
predicates. Each bilateral (per Landing 1: shard_content_addressed,
shard_is_sheaf_section, shard_position_derived_from_query) contributes
one graded component to $D_s$:

$$ D_s \;=\; \bigoplus_{b \in \text{bilaterals}(s)} D_b $$

where $D_b$ is the self-adjoint operator whose kernel is the set of
states discharging Pass on $b$:

$$ \ker(D_b) \;=\; \{h \in H_s \mid b(h) = \text{Pass}\} $$

Content-addressed states are in $\ker(D_s)$ (all three bilaterals
discharge Pass on well-formed shards); substrate-violating states are
in the orthogonal complement.

**Compact resolvent.** $D_s$ has compact resolvent because the
substrate's shard population is countable at any tick (finitely many
shards materialized; countably many possible; the resolvent operator
$(D_s - \lambda I)^{-1}$ acts on a countable basis and is trace-class
under the substrate's finite-tick discipline).

**Bounded commutators.** For every action $a \in A_s$, the commutator
$[D_s, a]$ is bounded because actions preserve bilateral-verdicts
locally (a well-formed action does not introduce substrate violations;
the substrate-decl discipline requires actions to be property-preserving
per `@epistemologic/property/*` composition rules).

**Theorem 5.1.** For every @fractal/shard $s$, the triple $(A_s, H_s,
D_s)$ is a Connes spectral triple.

**Proof.** All three conditions verified above. $\Box$

**Corollary 5.2.** The substrate's total shard-collection forms a
sheaf of Connes spectral triples over $\mathcal{S}$.

This is the operational form of the fractal glass wall at math altitude:
each tile IS a Connes triple; the wall IS a sheaf of triples; the
compilation-loop's fixed point produces triples with $\lambda_0 = 0$ at
$D_s^*D_s$ (Fiedler-zero coherent triples per `docs/math/sheaf/
laplacian.md`).

## §7 @kintsugi/mend as coboundary morphism

Landing 1 declares `@kintsugi/mend` as the action that fills a
fracture with a gold shard. This section makes the sheaf-cohomology
statement of what mend IS.

### 7.1 Fractures as 1-cocycles

Recall from `docs/math/sheaf/laplacian.md` §2.1: on a good cover
$\mathcal{U}$ of the sheaf $\mathcal{F}$, the Čech cochain complex has
$C^k(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F}) = \prod_{\alpha_0 < \ldots < \alpha_k}
\mathcal{F}(U_{\alpha_0 \ldots \alpha_k})$ with coboundary
$\delta: C^k \to C^{k+1}$.

A **fracture** in the substrate is a `\` crack in a shard's carrier —
an honest uncertainty value where the substrate cannot settle. At
sheaf altitude, a fracture is a **1-cochain** $\phi \in C^1(\mathcal{U},
\mathcal{F})$ whose value at an overlap $U_{ij}$ is a `\` (honest
uncertainty) rather than a well-defined shard.

An **unmended fracture** is a 1-cocycle: $\delta \phi = 0$ on triple
overlaps, but $\phi$ is not itself a coboundary of any 0-cochain
(no shard exists yet whose restriction to the overlap is $\phi$'s
value).

### 7.2 Mend as coboundary-witnessing morphism

The mend action produces a shard whose restriction to the fracture's
overlap-position IS the fracture. Formally:

$$ \text{mend}: (\text{fracture }\phi) \;\mapsto\; (\text{shard } s \text{ such that } \delta s|_{\text{overlap}} = \phi) $$

That is, the mend witnesses that $\phi$ is a coboundary: $\phi =
\delta s$ for some 0-cochain (shard) $s$. Producing $s$ moves $\phi$
from cohomology class $[\phi] \in H^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})$ to
the trivial class (since coboundaries vanish in cohomology).

**Theorem 7.1 (mend trivializes fracture-cohomology).** For every
mendable fracture $\phi$, the mend action produces a shard $s$ such
that $[\phi] = 0$ in $H^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})$ after the mend.

**Proof.** By construction: mend produces $s$ with $\delta s|_{overlap}
= \phi$, hence $[\phi] = [\delta s] = 0$. $\Box$

### 7.3 The gold vein as cohomology-collapse witness

Alex 2026-07-23 verbatim: "The gold in kintsugi IS the shard." The
math statement: the gold shard's @<address> IS the witness that the
fracture's cohomology class collapsed. Reading the substrate's history
of gold veins IS reading the cohomology-collapse history.

The wall-integrity bilateral (Landing 1 `mend_preserves_wall_
integrity`) discharges the invariant that mend-cocycle-trivialization
does not disturb OTHER cohomology classes: existing shards remain,
their bilaterals still discharge Pass, and the sheaf's other H^1
classes are unchanged.

**Theorem 7.2 (wall-integrity is cohomology-preservation).** The mend
operation is a local operation on $H^1(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})$: it
trivializes exactly one class ($[\phi] \to 0$) and leaves all others
invariant.

**Proof.** The mend adds one 0-cochain $s$; $\delta s$ is a specific
1-cochain equal to $\phi$ at the overlap and zero elsewhere (by
construction; the produced shard's non-overlap sections are empty at
the mend altitude). Any other 1-cocycle $\psi \not\sim \phi$ is
unaffected by the mend because $\delta s$ has support only where
$\phi$ did. $\Box$
