## §3. Four-criteria evaluation for `@knife`

### 3.1 Typed surface — parametric species per target type

**Verdict: FITS (would-fit-if-landed, high confidence).**

Mara §7.5.4 already sketches: `@knife[text]`, `@knife[mq]`,
`@knife[diff]`. Adding the L-cascade evidence: `@knife[altitude]`
species per specificity-frame. This is the same shape as `@shatter[T]`
Mara adopted in §7.5.3 (Option B: typed carrier at compile time).

Precedent siblings for parametric-species surfaces:

- **`@code/<lang>`** (rust, elixir, python, …) — parametric over
  target language.
- **`@glue`'s per-altitude species** (@cascade/code/<src>/<tgt>,
  @spectral/metalogue/tomm, …) — parametric over morphism-kind.
- **`@arxiv/math/<paper>`** — parametric over cited paper.
- **`@meta` markers** across the substrate — one axis of parameterization.

The typed-surface criterion is EXTREMELY well-precedented across
the substrate. `@knife[T]` would follow the exact same pattern.

**Grade: A** (would-fit clean).

### 3.2 Unified engine — one physics underneath

**Verdict: MIXED.** The claim depends on what the engine IS.

For `@shatter`, Mara §6 argues the engine is `@fate` (D²NN + Reck-
Clements mesh per Recognition #58). That's the load-bearing
"unified engine" claim.

For `@knife`, the candidate engines are:

- **`@fate`** (via kintsugi geometry on `crack`; `@knife` cuts
  produce fractures; `@kintsugi` closes them; `@fate` picks the
  candidate closure). This gives `@knife` the SAME engine as
  `@shatter`.
- **`@kintsugi`** (the transformation family-root; the process-
  side substrate). This gives `@knife` a DIFFERENT engine than
  `@shatter` — one that's still substrate-family-root, but at
  the process-side rather than the inference-side.
- **Direct substrate cut (no engine).** Linear-linear operations
  MAY not need a physics — a text cut is deterministic given the
  cursor. Under this reading, `@knife` has no unified engine because
  it doesn't NEED one.

The mixed verdict matters. Mara §7.5.4 explicitly hedges:

> Whether this fits or forces something different (e.g., `@knife` is
> truly one-sorted because linear-linear composition has no
> cross-type coherence problem) is a Pack question.

The scout's read: **`@knife` probably has a unified engine at IDF
altitude (where it needs @fate to select which cut to make from an
underdetermined set of candidate cuts) but NOT at the elementary
scissor altitude (where the cut is deterministic given a cursor).**
The engine question is altitude-parametric.

**Grade: C+ at family-root altitude; A at `@knife/idf(altitude)`
altitude.**

### 3.3 `@duality` present — parametric-vs-opaque equivalence

**Verdict: PARTIALLY FITS.**

For `@shatter`, `@duality` witnesses the equivalence between the
compile-time typed `@shatter[T]` and the runtime opaque `shatter`
(Mara §7.5.2). The parametric fibre (the family) equals the opaque
carrier (the runtime unified `@fate` inference) under `@duality`.

For `@knife`, does the same equivalence hold?

- **Parametric side:** `@knife[text]` / `@knife[mq]` / `@knife[diff]`
  — a family of typed scissors.
- **Opaque side:** a single `knife` runtime carrier that dispatches
  to the appropriate cut based on payload type.

The equivalence would say: `@knife[T]` at compile time = `knife`
at runtime, mediated by `@duality`. This is structurally identical
to the `@shatter[T]` case.

**BUT:** the L-cascade evidence complicates it. If `@knife` carries
an altitude parameter (per jspace §5: `@knife/idf(altitude)`), then
the parametric surface is 2-dimensional (T × altitude), not 1D. The
`@duality` witness would need to lift to both dimensions. Not
impossible — the substrate's `mosaic(altitude)` composition already
does this — but structurally MORE than `@shatter`'s single-dimension
`@duality`.

**Grade: B** (fits but heavier than `@shatter`'s witness).

### 3.4 `@magic` dispatch — mechanism/reveal discharge

**Verdict: PARTIALLY FITS.**

`@magic`'s structure at `shards/magic.mirror` + species:

- `magic_surface` = the gauge-visible interface (5-op signature).
- `magic_mechanism` = the matter-hidden trick (encapsulated).
- `magic_contract` = the promise binding surface to mechanism.
- Species: surface, mechanism, contract, reveal, audit, frame,
  distinction.

For `@shatter`, Mara §7.5 argues `@magic` discharges the compile-
to-runtime dispatch: `@shatter[T]` (gauge surface) is bound to
`shatter` (matter mechanism) by a `shatter_magic` contract; the
observer sees the typed `@shatter[T]` interface while `@fate`
runs behind the encapsulation.

For `@knife`, does `@magic` discharge equivalently?

- **`@knife[text]` as surface** — gauge-visible scissor interface
  parametric over target linear type.
- **Underlying `knife` engine as mechanism** — the actual byte-level
  cut logic (may or may not consult `@fate`).
- **`knife_magic` contract** — the promise that the surface's cut
  spec is delivered by the mechanism.

Structurally, this WORKS. The `@magic/reveal` species would
discharge the runtime dispatch atomically (per Recognition #81's
hot-reload framing); `@magic/audit` would verify the contract
holds; `@magic/mechanism` would encapsulate the actual cut.

BUT — and this is the sharpness — `@knife`'s cuts are OFTEN
straightforward-deterministic (text[0..n]). `@magic` is
architecturally about **matter-hidden mechanism behind gauge
surface**. If the mechanism is transparent (no hiding), then
`@magic` is decorative at best and vacuous at worst. Matches
Recognition #80's own failure mode ("some non-form/process
substrate concept also fits `@magic`'s shape, meaning the name
is too broad").

**Grade: B- at elementary cut altitude (`@magic` fits but adds no
observability the substrate wasn't providing); A at `@knife/idf`
altitude (where the specificity-frame IS matter-hidden and the
gauge-visible surface IS the returned specificity vector).**

## §5. Meta-pattern verdict + falsification signal

### On `@knife`: PARTIAL FIT.

At the elementary linear-linear scissor altitude, `@knife` has
typed surface + weak unified engine + `@duality`-shaped equivalence
+ decorative `@magic`. Two of four criteria strong; two weak.

At the `@knife/idf(altitude)` altitude (the L-cascade site),
`@knife` has typed surface + strong unified engine (`@fate` picks
which fragments to weight) + strong `@duality` (parametric idf
altitude vs opaque specificity vector) + strong `@magic`
(specificity is genuinely matter-hidden; the frame IS the
mechanism). All four criteria satisfied at this altitude.

**The meta-pattern is altitude-parametric.** `@knife` fits at
`@knife/idf(altitude)` altitude; `@knife` is decorative at
elementary-scissor altitude.

### On the meta-pattern's status:

- **`@shatter`** — full fit (Mara §7.5).
- **`@knife`** — partial fit (altitude-dependent).
- **`@glue`** — full fit (strongest second witness).
- **`@kintsugi`** — does not fit currently (unified engine only).
- **`@bauchladen`** — does not fit (no matter-hidden).

**Result:** two-of-five witness families support the meta-pattern
(`@shatter`, `@glue`). One partial (`@knife`, altitude-parametric).
Two negative (`@kintsugi`, `@bauchladen`).

**Per Mara's promotion criterion** ("If Seam finds the factorization
applies to at least two additional prism families cleanly, the
meta-pattern earns candidate-recognition status"), the scout finds
**ONE additional clean factoring (`@glue`) plus one partial
(`@knife`)**. That is enough for Seam adjudication of candidate-
recognition status but weaker than a clean two-witness promotion.

### Falsification signal (what would kill the meta-pattern):

The `@kintsugi` + `@bauchladen` negative results are the sharpest
falsification data. Both have unified engines but neither has the
parametric surface + matter-hidden mechanism combo. This suggests
the meta-pattern is not "every prism family with (typed surface) +
(unified engine) factors as @X × @duality × @magic" but rather:

> **`@X × @duality × @magic` factors those prism families whose
> engine is UNDERDETERMINED — where the parametric surface names
> the underdetermination space and the engine picks among typed
> candidates.**

Under this refined statement:
- `@shatter` — YES, parse/render is underdetermined at the graph/
  linear coupling.
- `@glue` — YES, morphism selection is underdetermined within a
  restriction (this is literally the shard's load-bearing claim).
- `@knife/idf(altitude)` — YES, specificity is underdetermined at
  a given altitude.
- `@kintsugi` — NO, the flow is DETERMINED (contraction).
- `@bauchladen` — NO, content-addressing is DETERMINED (byte-equal).

This refined statement makes the meta-pattern more precise AND
predicts which families it applies to. Seam should press on this
refinement.
