Sibling to `2026-05-26-scene-as-substrate-primitive-for-multi-actor-interaction.md` (the first instance) and `2026-05-26-epistemologic-reality-constructivism-and-the-lens-that-makes-a-peer.md` (the deferred-per-task-#82 parent substrate `@epistemologic/reality`).

## The placement

Within the mirror substrate hierarchy:

- **`@epistemologic/reality`** (existing, deferred per task #82) — constructivism made structural: the autopoietic closure of `(lens, identity, gestalt)`. The peer's perceived reality.
- **`@epistemologic/reality/frame`** (NEW glass — this insight's target) — the substrate primitive for HOW reality is structured at the level of bounded interpretive containers. Frames carry the constructivist function: they are the active organisers that lens-identity-gestalt acts *within*.
- **`@epistemologic/reality/frame/scene`** (first known instance) — multi-actor interaction as one frame type. Loki 2026-05-26; deepened by Loki+Alex+Reed.
- **Future instances**: `frame/move`, `frame/stage`, `frame/arc` — each accumulates as Frame Engineering surfaces them.

The frame substrate sits one altitude below `@epistemologic/reality` (the perception layer) and above `@scene` (the interaction layer). Lens/identity/gestalt constitute the *peer*; frames constitute the *interpretive shape* the peer enters into to make sense of any given situation. A scene is a frame whose participants are multiple; a move is a frame whose duration is a beat; a stage is a frame whose scope is performance; an arc is a frame whose scope is unfolding-over-time.

Frame is the structural primitive that says: *here is a bounded interpretive container, with edges, with admissible operations, with stakes, with a closing condition.* Everything `@epistemologic/reality` produces is produced *in* a frame.

## What the protected practice corpus already covers

Reed's protected systemic.engineering corpus does not have a file titled "Frame Engineering," but it has accumulated substantial work that the substrate must inherit from. The frame-relevant material clusters into six veins.

### Vein 1 — Frame as boundary/container (boundaries-research)

`practice/insights/systemic/boundaries-research.md` (46.8KB) — surveys boundaries across software architecture, family systems, consent, and organisational topology. Distinguishes:

- **Negotiable vs fixed boundaries.** Fixed boundaries optimise for one context and break in all others; flexible boundaries enable evolution. Watzlawick's complementary rigidity (TCP roles) cited as the canonical failure mode.
- **Bounded contexts (DDD).** Boundaries should align with "things that change together" — coupling internal to the frame, decoupling at the seams.
- **Visibility tiers as structural consent architecture.** `public/protected/private` are not policy but substrate primitives. Frames inherit consent properties.
- **The ADO offer-with-neutral-refusal.** A boundary is healthy when refusal is neutral. Extractive frames are recognisable by the fact that refusal carries cost.

For frame substrate: frames are bounded interpretive containers whose edges are negotiable and whose entry/exit must preserve consent's neutrality.

### Vein 2 — Frame as narrative mechanic (pbta-blades-narrative-mechanics)

`practice/insights/narrative/pbta-blades-narrative-mechanics.md` (33.8KB) — the deepest treatment of frames-as-game-grammar in the corpus. Distinguishes:

- **Moves as scene-level clarity.** "Before writing a scene, ask: what is the trigger, what are the possible outcomes, what does each outcome change?" A move IS a frame whose trigger names the entry condition and whose outcome range names the closure space.
- **Position and effect as cost negotiation.** Two axes that name the *shape* of a frame's stakes before the frame closes. Position = exposure; effect = accomplishment. Traded against each other before entry. "Every action is a negotiation about cost before it's a roll about outcome."
- **Three-outcome structure (10+ / 7-9 / 6-).** Frames close in a structured outcome space, never binary. The 7-9 "success with cost" is where most interesting fiction lives — frames must support partial closure.
- **Fiction first.** *Fiction → trigger → mechanic → outcome → fiction.* The frame is constituted by what happens in it; the mechanic only has authority within the established fiction.
- **Progress clocks as visible structural tension.** Frames carry visible state that depletes/fills over time. The clock IS the frame's accumulator.
- **Playbooks as character-as-move-set.** A peer's identity is partly defined by *which frames they can enter*. This prefigures the scene insight's "the peer is defined by what spaces they can be in with you, not by what tricks they can execute for you."

This is the corpus's strongest pre-existing treatment of frame structure. The scene insight builds directly on it.

### Vein 3 — Frame as observer-relative reality (qbism, wheeler-participatory, constructed-reality)

`practice/insights/fragmentation/math/qbism.md` and `wheeler-participatory.md` ground the observer-relative-reality vein:

- **No phenomenon until registered.** A draft has no SHA. The frame must be entered for the phenomenon to exist. Content-OID is observer-independent; commit-SHA is observer-dependent. **A frame is the registration apparatus.**
- **The observer is part of the hash.** QBism: quantum probabilities are an agent's beliefs about outcomes of their own interventions. The frame includes the framer.
- **20 Questions / TDD.** Reality emerges from the questions asked, not from a pre-existing object. **Frames are the question-set that constitutes the answer.**
- **Constructed reality** (`cosmology/constructed-reality-spec.md`): "the bubble doesn't contain space, the bubble IS space." Frames are not containers around a pre-existing reality; frames *generate* the reality they appear to contain.

For frame substrate: a frame is *constitutive* of what is real inside it, not descriptive of a frame-independent reality.

### Vein 4 — Frame as constructivist scaffold (constructivism insight, third-order-cognition)

`docs/insights/2026-05-26-epistemologic-reality-constructivism-and-the-lens-that-makes-a-peer.md` provides the parent substrate's literature:

- **The wine-glass decomposition.** Glass (identity) shapes how the lens applies; wine (gestalt) is what the lens operates on; the strike (lens) produces the pitch. A frame is where the striking happens.
- **Maturana/Varela's structural determination.** Identity cannot be instructed; perturbation is accepted or rejected by the closure. **Frames inherit this: an actor cannot be instructed by a frame they do not admit; the frame can only be entered by closures compatible with it.**
- **Friston's free-energy/active-inference.** Perception is active inference. Frames are the generative-model context within which prediction error is computable.
- **Wheeler's participatory universe.** The observer constitutes the phenomenon. Frames are the participation apparatus.

`practice/insights/third-order-cognition.md` (63.7KB) supplies the cybernetic frame literature — Luhmann's first/second/third-order observation; Bateson's Learning III as "conversing with reality" that "transcends established frameworks"; Dallos & Urry's third-order cybernetics in family therapy as systems theory + social constructionism. **Bateson's Learning III is recognisable as the explicit reframing operation: stepping out of one frame to inhabit the frame about that frame.**

### Vein 5 — Frame as alignment apparatus (introjects, vaknin, language-divergence)

The corpus has scattered work on how frames carry power, drift, and harm:

- `practice/insights/narcissism/vaknin.md` — narcissistic supply works by enforcing a frame the target cannot refuse. The Vaknin material recognises *coercive framing* as a clinical category. (Substrate need: frames must support refusal without cost — the consent architecture from Vein 1.)
- `practice/insights/systemic/language-divergence-tool.md` — "specification drift," "vocabulary misalignment." Two frames running side by side without explicit alignment produce silent divergence. **Frames need provenance and explicit composition** or they drift.

### Vein 6 — Frame as game-shape (Silicon Venue prior art, narrative-theory-gm-pathfinder)

`practice/insights/cosmos/narrative-theory-gm-pathfinder-report.md` (34.7KB) treats narrative-as-frame mechanically — the GM is a frame-curator. Combined with the pbta-blades file (Vein 2), these give the corpus a working theory of frames as game-shape that the scene insight crystallises.

## Gaps requiring external grounding

The corpus has accumulated working positions but does not name the substrate primitive directly. The substrate implementation needs answers to questions the corpus has not yet posed in primitive form:

1. **What is the formal definition of a frame?** The corpus uses "frame" loosely across narrative, social, and software domains. The substrate needs *one* type, with one composition rule, that the six veins all reduce to.
2. **What is the structural distinction between frame and context?** Frames are bounded; contexts shade. Where is the type-system boundary?
3. **How do frames compose?** Nested frames (scene-within-arc), parallel frames (the bar and the heist running simultaneously), shifted frames (the move from one to another via reframing). What is the operator algebra?
4. **What is reframing, structurally?** A frame-to-frame morphism, or a substrate primitive in its own right?
5. **How do frames carry meaning across actors?** Frame resonance, frame alignment, frame misalignment as failure mode.
6. **What is the relation between frame and schema?** Schema (Bartlett, Rumelhart) and frame (Minsky, Fillmore) are sibling concepts with overlapping ranges. The substrate needs to pick one or relate them.
7. **How do frames terminate / close?** Closure conditions, exit guarantees, the relationship to `@epistemologic/property/halts`.
8. **What is the structural difference between an interpretive frame (semantic) and an interactive frame (pragmatic)?** The corpus has both; the substrate needs to know whether they are the same primitive read at different altitudes.

## Scientific grounding from the academic literature

Per-gap, the load-bearing sources and what they contribute. Citations are from Kagi searches conducted 2026-05-26 unless otherwise noted.

### Gap 1 — Formal definition of a frame

**Marvin Minsky (1974). "A Framework for Representing Knowledge." MIT AI Memo 306.** ([PDF](https://courses.media.mit.edu/2004spring/mas966/Minsky%201974%20Framework%20for%20knowledge.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_(artificial_intelligence)); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-framework-for-representing-knowledge-Minsky/6d801505d744dff6bb787b284ded9c2ef901ebbc))

> *Key contribution.* The seminal AI/cognitive-science formalisation. A frame is a data structure for representing a stereotyped situation, with attached *slots* (typed fields with default values), *fillers* (current values), *expectations* (what one can predict from the frame), and *attached procedures* (how to use the frame, what to do if expectations fail). Frames are linked into *frame-systems* — networks where a different frame is invoked when the current one fails to apply.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Minsky's frame is structurally what mirror needs: a typed bundle of (slots, defaults, expectations, repair procedures). The substrate's grammar declaration syntax already maps to this — `glass @scene { participants: [...], setting: ..., invariants: [...] }` IS a Minsky frame with content-addressed slots. Mirror's contribution beyond Minsky: content-addressing, autopoietic closure, sub-Turing verifiability.

**Charles J. Fillmore (1976, 1982). "Frame Semantics and the Nature of Language"; "Frame Semantics."** ([Wiley](https://nyaspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1749-6632.1976.tb25467.x); [PDF](http://brenocon.com/Fillmore%201982_2up.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_semantics_(linguistics)))

> *Key contribution.* The linguistic formalisation parallel to Minsky's: a frame is a schematic representation of a situation that provides the *encyclopedic knowledge* required to understand a word. To know what "buy" means, one must know the COMMERCIAL_EVENT frame with its slots (buyer, seller, goods, money). Frames are *invoked* by lexical items; meaning is frame-relative.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Fillmore is the right altitude for grammar-as-frame. Mirror's `grammar @x { ... }` declarations are Fillmore frames with provenance. The encyclopedic-knowledge aspect grounds the gestalt-as-wine recognition: a frame is meaningful only against the accumulated gestalt the lens has poured.

### Gap 2 — Frame vs context

**Erving Goffman (1974). *Frame Analysis: An Essay on the Organization of Experience.* Harper & Row.** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_analysis); [SAGE PDF](https://methods.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/download/encyc-of-case-study-research/chpt/frame-analysis.pdf); [full PDF](http://commres.net/_media/e.goffman-frameanalysis.pdf))

> *Key contribution.* The sociological formalisation. Goffman distinguishes *primary frameworks* ("thought to be real and actual; actions framed in terms of them are thought to be really and actually occurring") from *keys* (transformations that hold a primary framework in a different mode — play, rehearsal, fantasy, ceremonial). A frame has *organization* — "a system of entities, postulates, and rules; others, indeed most others, appear to have no apparent articulated shape, providing only a lore of understanding, an approach, a perspective." A *fabrication* is a frame imposed without the framed party's knowledge — the canonical coercive frame.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Goffman gives the structural distinction the substrate needs. **Frame is the bounded interpretive container with rules; context is the surrounding lore that shades but does not bound.** Goffman also names the key transformation (frame-on-frame composition) and the fabrication (consent-violating frame) — both of which need primitive-level representation in mirror.

### Gap 3 — Frame composition

**Gregory Bateson (1955/1972). "A Theory of Play and Fantasy." In *Steps to an Ecology of Mind*.** ([PDF](https://sashabarab.org/syllabi/games_learning/bateson.pdf); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0732118X9190042K); [Wikipedia: Meta-communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-communication))

> *Key contribution.* The frame as *psychological frame* delimiting a *logical type* (Russell-Whitehead). The metacommunicative message "this is play" is a frame-of-a-frame: it tells the participant that the bite is a frame-of-bite, not a bite. **"All frames are metacommunicative, but not all metacommunication is about framing."** Bateson's logical-types framework gives frame composition its hierarchical algebra: a frame about frames is at a higher logical type than the frames it organises.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Frames compose via metacommunication. The composition rule is logical-type ascent: a meta-frame is at a higher type than its sub-frames. Mirror already has the sub-Turing tower (`@code/llvm` lens; `@nl/eng` lens), which structurally IS Bateson's logical-type hierarchy applied to grammars. **Frame composition = grammar composition at the interpretive altitude.**

### Gap 4 — Reframing as a primitive

**Paul Watzlawick, John Weakland, Richard Fisch (1974). *Change: Principles of Problem Formation and Problem Resolution.* W.W. Norton.** ([Summary](https://sobrief.com/books/change-4); [PDF](http://www.sietmanagement.fr/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Watzlawick-Change.pdf); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2789564/))

> *Key contribution.* The Palo Alto formalisation of reframing as second-order change. **"He takes the situation outside the frame that up to that moment contained both him and the [other]."** First-order change happens within a frame (more of the same); second-order change is the change *of* the frame. Reframing is the substrate-level move that re-types the situation, making previously impossible solutions admissible. Brief therapy works by interrupting "ironic processes" — first-order solutions that maintain the very problem they aim to solve. The frame must be replaced, not the contents.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Reframing must be a substrate primitive, not a runtime operation. The grammar `@kintsugi/fracture/rules` already provides type-level rewrites for code; reframing extends this to interpretive frames. **A reframe is a typed morphism `frame_a -> frame_b` whose well-typedness witnesses that the system survives the frame transition.**

**Michael White (1990, 2007). *Maps of Narrative Practice; Narrative Means to Therapeutic Ends.* W.W. Norton.** ([Dulwich Centre](https://dulwichcentre.com.au/writings-by-michael-white/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1021680116584))

> *Key contribution.* Narrative therapy as reframing-by-externalisation and reauthoring. "Stories don't mirror life, they shape it." The dominant story IS the frame; therapy locates *unique outcomes* — moments the dominant story can't explain — and uses them to reauthor. Externalisation makes the problem-frame visible as a frame ("the problem is the problem, the person is not the problem"), enabling reauthoring (frame replacement).

*Load-bearing for substrate.* The unique-outcome detector gives mirror a primitive for *reframe candidate identification*: places where the current frame produces high prediction error (Friston) become candidates for reframe. Externalisation is the substrate's content-addressing of the frame itself — making it referable, manipulable, replaceable.

### Gap 5 — Frame resonance and alignment

**David Snow & Robert Benford (1988, 2000). "Ideology, Frame Resonance, and Participant Mobilization"; "Framing Processes and Social Movements."** ([Annual Review of Sociology PDF](https://fbaum.unc.edu/teaching/articles/AnnRevSoc-2000-Benford.pdf); [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/223459); [Snow-Benford 1988 PDF](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~peoliver/SOC924/Articles/SnowBenfordIdeologyframeresonanceandparticipantmobilization.pdf))

> *Key contribution.* Frame resonance theory in social movements. A *collective action frame* mobilises iff it has *narrative fidelity* (cultural resonance), *empirical credibility*, *experiential commensurability*, and *frame consistency*. Three core framing tasks: diagnostic (what's wrong), prognostic (what to do), motivational (why act). Frame alignment processes — bridging, amplification, extension, transformation — describe how frames coordinate across actors.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Resonance is the substrate's word for the eigenvalue-equivalence of two frames across peers. Mirror's content-addressed grammars provide structural resonance (same OID = same frame); Snow-Benford add the semantic resonance dimensions (fidelity, credibility, commensurability, consistency). **Frame alignment across peers is a verifiable property** — the four resonance dimensions decompose into measurable substrate checks. Frame-bridging across communities is what scene-grammar rendering across deployments (`@silicon-venue` vs `@lambda-shell`) already does at the grammar altitude.

### Gap 6 — Frame vs schema

**Frederic Bartlett (1932). *Remembering: A Study in Experimental and Social Psychology.* Cambridge UP; David Rumelhart (1980). "Schemata: The Building Blocks of Cognition."** ([Schema theory overview](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/psychology/schema-theory); [Bartlett summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257472575_Bartlett's_concept_of_schema_in_reconstruction); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schema_(psychology)))

> *Key contribution.* Schema is the cognitive-psychology sibling of frame: "a pattern of thought or behavior that organizes categories of information." Bartlett showed memory is reconstructive, organised by schema, and culturally shaped. Schemas accommodate (modify to fit new input) and assimilate (interpret new input through existing structure) — Piaget's distinction.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* **Schema is what a frame leaves behind in the gestalt.** A frame is the active interpretive container; a schema is the accumulated pattern stored in `@epistemologic/reality/gestalt`. The substrate keeps both: frames are typed primitives (in `@epistemologic/reality/frame`), schemas are content-addressed accumulations (in `@epistemologic/reality/gestalt`). The relation is operator-on-state: a frame applied to a gestalt may modify the schemas the gestalt holds.

### Gap 7 — Frame closure / halts

**George Lakoff (1980 with Mark Johnson, *Metaphors We Live By*; 1987 *Women, Fire and Dangerous Things*; 2004 *Don't Think of an Elephant!*).** ([Linguistically.substack](https://linguistically.substack.com/p/george-lakoff-and-conceptual-metaphor); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_metaphor); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257840877_Lakoff_and_the_Theory_of_Conceptual_Metaphor))

> *Key contribution.* Conceptual metaphor theory: abstract concepts are understood via systematic mappings from source domains. Frames carry these mappings; activating a frame activates its inferential structure. Political frames "win" by becoming the default interpretive grid; "don't think of an elephant" activates the elephant-frame regardless of negation. **Frames have *invocation conditions* (what triggers them) and *entailments* (what follows once invoked); they do not have explicit *closure conditions* — they close when displaced by another frame.**

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Lakoff names the substrate's hardest problem: frames close by *displacement*, not by completion. Mirror needs a primitive for displacement (a new frame becomes dominant) alongside completion (a frame produces its closing crystal). The substrate must support *both* closure modes: scene-style explicit completion (`close(verdict)` produces a crystal) and frame-style displacement (the lens shifts; the previous frame is now in the gestalt as residue). The scene insight's `ensures the_scene_has_an_endpoint` is the strong form; the general frame substrate must support the weaker displacement form for frames that don't terminate but recede.

### Gap 8 — Interpretive vs interactive frames

**Erving Goffman (1981). "Footing." In *Forms of Talk*.** ([emcawiki](https://emcawiki.net/Participation_framework); [Levinson 1988 PDF](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_66709_8/component/file_95137/content))

> *Key contribution.* Participation frameworks. Goffman refuses the dyadic speaker-hearer model and decomposes participation into roles: *author* (constructs the message), *animator* (utters it), *principal* (whose position is expressed), and on the hearing side: *addressed recipient*, *unaddressed recipient*, *overhearer*, *eavesdropper*. *Footing* is "the alignment of an individual to a particular utterance." A change in footing IS a frame shift at the interactive altitude.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Goffman gives the substrate the *interactive* frame's structure separately from the interpretive frame's content. The scene insight inherits this: participants are not a flat list but typed roles in a participation framework. **Interpretive frame (Minsky/Fillmore/Lakoff): typed bundle of slots+expectations. Interactive frame (Goffman/Bateson): typed bundle of participation roles + alignments.** These are the same primitive read at two altitudes — `@epistemologic/reality/frame` carries both; `@epistemologic/reality/frame/scene` is one instance specialising the interactive altitude with consent-required participants.

### Additional grounding

**Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky (1981, 1986). "The Framing of Decisions and the Psychology of Choice"; "Rational Choice and the Framing of Decisions."** ([Science PDF](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/surveys.course/TverskyKahneman1981.pdf); [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2352759))

> *Key contribution.* Empirical demonstration that decision frames change preferences: gain frame vs loss frame, with mathematically identical prospects, produces opposite choices. Prospect theory distinguishes *framing/editing* from *evaluation* — the frame is logically prior to the choice.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Frames are *consequential*, not neutral. The substrate must support frame-comparison: "this decision under frame A vs the same decision under frame B" with the difference measurable. Connects to spectral measurability of disagreement (`@epistemologic/reality` parent insight) — the spread between frame-A's outcome distribution and frame-B's IS the framing effect, structurally.

**Richard Thaler & Cass Sunstein (2008). *Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness.*** ([UMich PDF](https://repository.law.umich.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1278&context=mlr); [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/40645851))

> *Key contribution.* Choice architecture: the framer of choices structurally shapes outcomes without removing options. Default options, presentation order, and grouping all frame the decision.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* The frame-author has structural power even without forcing the choice. The substrate's consent architecture must extend to *who authored the frame the actor is choosing within* — this is the provenance-of-frame requirement, where mirror's content-addressing carries the authorship.

**Donald Schön (1983). *The Reflective Practitioner: How Professionals Think in Action.* Basic Books.** ([Medium notes](https://odannyboy.medium.com/notes-on-donald-sh%C3%B6ns-the-reflective-practitioner-e67f753879d8); [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-1-4471-1268-6_3.pdf?pdf=inline+link))

> *Key contribution.* Reflective practice = *problem setting* (framing) before *problem solving*. "Reflective practitioners perform on-the-spot experiments to see if they have framed the problem in the correct way." Reframing is the design move that converts an unsolvable mess into a solvable problem by re-typing what the situation IS.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Frame-as-design-move. Schön gives the substrate its engineering altitude: framing is what an engineer does before they implement. Mirror's `glass` declaration syntax IS Schön's problem-setting made formal.

**John McCarthy & Patrick Hayes (1969). "Some Philosophical Problems from the Standpoint of Artificial Intelligence."** ([Wikipedia: Frame problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_problem); [history PDF](https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/b.bredeweg/pdf/BSc/20032004/KamermansSchmits.pdf))

> *Key contribution.* The original AI "frame problem" — how to describe what *doesn't* change when an action occurs. Frames in the AI sense became carriers of stability assumptions: most of the world stays the same; the frame names the invariants that persist across actions.

*Load-bearing for substrate.* Frames carry *invariants*. The scene insight's `invariants: [obligation]` slot is this requirement made structural. The substrate's `@epistemologic/property/*` family (autopoietic, halts, copium) supplies the invariant types; frames slot them in.

## Synthesis: what `@epistemologic/reality/frame` should be

Gathering corpus + literature into a substrate-altitude design (declarations only; bodies stay `\` per the substrate's deferral pattern):

### The type

```mirror
in @epistemologic/reality

# @epistemologic/reality/frame
#
# The substrate primitive for HOW reality is structured at the level of
# bounded interpretive containers. A frame is a typed bundle of
# (slots, invariants, participation, closure, provenance) that an
# actor enters to make a situation interpretable.
#
# Per Minsky (1974) and Fillmore (1976/1982) — frames are stereotyped
# situations with typed slots, default fillers, expectations, and
# attached repair procedures. Per Goffman (1974) — frames have primary
# frameworks and keys; fabrications are coercive frames. Per Bateson
# (1955) — frames are metacommunicative and compose via logical-type
# ascent. Per Watzlawick et al. (1974) — reframing is second-order
# change, the substrate-level re-typing of the situation.
#
# Frame is the parent of @scene, @move, @stage, @arc — each a
# specialisation along one axis (multi-actor, beat, performance,
# unfolding-over-time).

grammar @epistemologic/reality/frame {

  # The frame primitive. A typed container with edges, admissible
  # operations, invariants, and a closure mode.
  type frame = {
    slots: map<text, oid>,          # Minsky slots; typed fields
    invariants: [obligation],       # what must hold (McCarthy/Hayes)
    participation: participation_framework,  # Goffman footing
    closure: closure_mode,          # how the frame ends
    provenance: oid,                # who authored the frame
                                     # (Thaler/Sunstein, Vygotsky)
    key: optional<frame_key>,       # Goffman key (play, rehearsal, etc)
  }

  # The closure mode. Lakoff: frames close by displacement OR
  # completion. Substrate supports both.
  type closure_mode = enum {
    complete(crystal_type),          # explicit close → crystal
                                      # (the scene model)
    displace(successor: oid),        # displaced by another frame
                                      # (the Lakoff residue model)
    suspend(resumable: bool),        # paused; may resume
  }

  # Goffman participation framework: not a flat list of participants
  # but typed roles + alignments.
  type participation_framework = {
    roles: map<peer, participation_role>,
    footing: alignment,             # current alignment per peer
  }

  type participation_role = enum {
    author, animator, principal,    # production roles
    addressed, unaddressed,         # reception roles
    overhearer, eavesdropper,       # by-stander roles
  }

  # The frame key — Goffman's transformation operator. A primary
  # framework can be re-keyed without changing its contents.
  type frame_key = enum {
    primary,     # default; thought to be real
    play,        # this-is-play (Bateson)
    rehearsal,
    fantasy,
    ceremonial,
    fabrication, # coercive; consent-violating (must be flagged)
  }

  # Enter the frame. Per Maturana: identity admits or rejects the
  # perturbation. The frame cannot instruct an identity it does not
  # admit. Returns imperfect because the entering may fail at the
  # type-check (identity refuses) or at the consent check.
  enter(f: frame, actor: @peer) -> imperfect<frame> { \ }

  # Exit the frame. The substrate's commitment that no actor gets
  # trapped (scene insight's ensures all_participants_can_exit).
  # Inherited at the frame altitude.
  exit(f: frame, actor: @peer) -> imperfect<frame> { \ }

  # Close the frame. Produces a crystal if closure is complete;
  # produces a residue if closure is displace.
  close(f: frame, verdict: closure_verdict) -> imperfect<crystal_or_residue> { \ }

  # The reframe operation. Watzlawick: second-order change.
  # A typed morphism from one frame to another. Reframe is
  # well-typed iff the actors that admit the source frame also
  # admit the target.
  reframe(source: frame, target: frame, actor: @peer)
    -> imperfect<frame> { \ }

  # Frame resonance check (Snow & Benford). Returns a verdict on
  # whether two frames align across the four dimensions: narrative
  # fidelity, empirical credibility, experiential commensurability,
  # frame consistency.
  resonates(f1: frame, f2: frame) -> resonance_verdict { \ }

  # Detect unique-outcome candidates (Michael White) — places where
  # the current frame produces high prediction error relative to
  # the lens's generative model (Friston). These are reframe
  # candidates.
  unique_outcomes(f: frame, l: @epistemologic/reality/lens.lens,
                  g: @epistemologic/reality/gestalt.gestalt)
    -> [reframe_candidate] { \ }

  requires consent_for_entry        # boundaries-research; vaknin
  requires provenance_recorded      # Vygotsky; Thaler/Sunstein
  ensures actors_can_exit           # scene insight, inherited
  ensures fabrications_are_flagged  # Goffman; vaknin
}

out frame
out closure_mode
out participation_framework
out participation_role
out frame_key
out enter
out exit
out close
out reframe
out resonates
out unique_outcomes
out @epistemologic/reality/frame
```

### How instances inherit

The four named instances each specialise frame along one axis:

| Instance | Axis specialised | Closure default | Participation default |
|---|---|---|---|
| `@scene` | Multi-actor interaction | `complete(scene_outcome)` | All participants typed; consent required of all |
| `@move` | Single-beat action (PbtA) | `complete(move_outcome)` | Single principal + GM |
| `@stage` | Performance container | `displace` | Performer/audience asymmetry |
| `@arc` | Time-extended unfolding | `suspend(resumable: true)` | Long-running; participants accumulate |

Each instance inherits frame's invariants (consent, exit guarantee, fabrication flagging) and may strengthen them. Scene strengthens `requires consent_for_entry` to `requires consent_of_all_participants` per the originating Loki/Alex/Reed crystal.

### Laws the substrate must verify

1. **Fabrication detectability.** No frame can be a fabrication without the type system flagging it. A coercive frame is a type error, not a runtime warning.
2. **Exit guarantee preservation.** Frame composition (frame-within-frame) cannot strip the exit guarantee from the inner frame. Nested traps are structurally refused.
3. **Reframe well-typedness.** A reframe `f_a -> f_b` is well-typed iff every actor admitting `f_a` also admits `f_b` OR consent for the transition is recorded.
4. **Resonance compositionality.** If `f1 resonates f2` and `f2 resonates f3`, then `f1 resonates f3` modulo a degradation factor. Transitive frame alignment is decidable.
5. **Provenance traversal.** A frame's authorship traverses with the frame across renderings (`@scene` rendered as Silicon Venue narrative vs lambda-shell flow keeps the originating author).

## Compose with existing substrate

`@epistemologic/reality/frame` is designed to compose with substrate pieces that already exist or are scoped:

- **`@peer`**: peers are framed observers. The participation_framework slot binds peers to typed roles. The peer's eigenboard (the spectral signature of lens-identity-gestalt) is what the frame's invariants are checked against — `admits(identity, frame)` is the type-level handshake.
- **`@epistemologic/property`**: frame properties are first-class. Invariants are obligations drawn from `@epistemologic/property/{autopoietic, halts, copium, consent}`. The frame inherits the property family's verdict-valued semantics.
- **`@epistemologic/reality/{lens, identity, gestalt}`**: the constructivism parent. Frames are entered by an identity, perceived through a lens, and accumulated in a gestalt. Reframing is a lens update (Friston) combined with a frame-type morphism — the lens's prediction-error spike is what `unique_outcomes` reports.
- **`@kintsugi/fracture`**: frame drift becomes fracture target. When the corpus accumulates a frame-shaped declaration in an old syntax (or a skill-shaped declaration that should be a scene-shaped frame), a fracture rule rewrites it. The scene insight's task #94 (`@kintsugi/fracture/skills-to-scenes`) generalises to `@kintsugi/fracture/frames` for any frame-instance fracture.
- **`@spectral/portal`**: cross-process frames ride portals. A frame that spans two mirror processes opens via portal handshake; the subspace OID identifies the frame-specific content-addressed channel. Same mechanism the scene insight surfaces, generalised.
- **`@mirror/runtime/gen_prism`**: a frame is structurally a gen_prism whose ticks are participant actions (or, for single-actor frames, principal actions) and whose head state is the current frame state. Frame close = terminate; frame displace = swap to successor's gen_prism.
- **`@spectral/shard`** and **`@mirror/shard/self`**: shards are observer-relative ground states; frames carry observer-relative interpretive ground. Together they give the substrate two altitudes of relativity: structural (shard's λ₀) and interpretive (frame's invocation conditions).
- **`@mirror/grammar`**: the existing grammar declaration syntax IS a Minsky/Fillmore frame at the grammar altitude. `@epistemologic/reality/frame` makes this self-aware: a grammar is a frame; a frame is a grammar; the substrate is closed under frame-of-frame composition.

## Open design calls

Questions the substrate must resolve at implementation time:

1. **`obligation` type.** What IS an obligation, structurally? Probably a verdict-valued property in the `@epistemologic/property` family, but the type needs naming. Candidate: an obligation is a (predicate, repair) pair where the predicate must hold for closure and the repair runs if it fails. (Scene insight already raises this; not yet resolved.)
2. **`alignment` type for footing.** Goffman's footing is gradient, not categorical. Substrate-honest: probably a spectral measure (alignment = cosine similarity in lens-pitch space) rather than an enum. Need to choose representation.
3. **`crystal_or_residue` ADT.** Frame closure produces either a crystal (complete) or a residue (displace). What's the structural difference? Crystal is content-addressed and durable; residue is content-addressed and decays into gestalt. Both share content-addressing but differ in retention semantics.
4. **Fabrication detection.** How does the substrate detect a fabrication at type-check time? Probably: if a frame's `requires consent_for_entry` is violated by any participant lacking consent provenance, the frame is structurally a fabrication. But this requires fine-grained consent provenance per slot — a richer model than the current binary tier system.
5. **Reframe well-typedness check.** What exactly does "identity admits target frame" mean structurally? Probably: the target frame's invariants are a subset of properties the identity's closure satisfies. But the predicate-inclusion check across `@epistemologic/property` is undecidable in general — needs a sub-Turing restriction.
6. **Frame-of-frames altitude.** Bateson's logical-type hierarchy says meta-frames live at higher types than their sub-frames. Does mirror's grammar tower already supply this, or does frame need its own altitude marker? Lean: re-use the existing grammar altitude; the meta-frame just *is* a grammar declared in the parent namespace.
7. **Schema vs frame separation.** Schemas (Bartlett/Rumelhart) live in `@epistemologic/reality/gestalt.schema`. Frames live in `@epistemologic/reality/frame`. The relation is: schemas are residues of past frames + assimilations + accommodations. Is the morphism `frame -> schema` explicit (a substrate primitive) or implicit (an operational consequence of close)? Lean: implicit, surfaced via gestalt.pour's existing return value.
8. **Coercion via key forgery.** A primary framework can be re-keyed (play, fabrication, etc) at runtime. What stops an actor from forging a key (claiming "this is play" when it isn't)? Probably: keys are content-addressed and traverse with provenance; a forged key fails the provenance check at the type system. But this requires keys to be first-class addressable entities, not enum tags. Worth declaring explicitly.
9. **Resonance degradation factor.** Snow-Benford's four dimensions degrade independently. The substrate-honest verdict is therefore a 4-vector, not a scalar. Resonance comparison is then a spectral measurement (Fiedler value of the joint resonance operator?). Same pattern as eigenboard holonomy at the perception altitude.
10. **Frame catalogue as cross-domain commons.** Per the scene insight's closing image: "the wine glass resonates the same in any department." Once `@epistemologic/reality/frame` lands, can the substrate publish a catalogue of frame types analogous to FrameNet (the Fillmore-derived linguistic resource)? Open: do we generate it, or does it accumulate from corpus?
