## 1. Position — what Alex asked and what this document tests

Alex 2026-06-26 (verbatim):

> What if we spawned Mara on a Kagi + Corpus research on psychohistory?
> And what if a psychohistory vector is a sheaf and what if the tomology
> of the work is the composed psychohistory?

Two hypotheses, both load-bearing for what follows, both tested against the
substrate's own development trajectory rather than against an external
empirical dataset.

**Hypothesis A — the sheaf hypothesis.** A *psychohistory vector*
`ψ(state, time)` IS a section of a sheaf `F` over the substrate's
development manifold `M`. Local pulls — single substrate-pull recognitions,
single Pack-tick decisions, single banking commits — compose to global
development direction via the sheaf's restriction maps. The local-to-global
problem of trajectory prediction IS, structurally, the local-to-global
problem of sheaf section extension.

**Hypothesis B — the cohomology hypothesis.** The *topology/cohomology* of
the work IS the composed psychohistory. Where Asimov's psychohistory was
imagined as a function `ψ: state × time → R^n` returning a probability
distribution over collective futures, the substrate's psychohistory is the
cohomology `H*(M, F)` of its own development sheaf. Sections that fail to
extend globally — recognitions that DON'T compose, candidates that DON'T
ratify, branches that DON'T merge — are the cohomology classes. Sections
that DO extend globally — the cascade as a whole — are the sheaf's global
sections.

The two hypotheses are not independent. If A holds, B is what A's
mathematical machinery already commits to: sheaves admit cohomology by
construction. If A fails — if no sheaf structure earns its lines on the
substrate's manifold — B fails with it.

What this document tests, then, is not "does psychohistory work" (Asimov's
question, Turchin's question, the question every social science asks). It
tests "does psychohistory have the SAME SHAPE as what the substrate is
operationally doing when it watches itself develop." If yes, the
substrate-pull-honest move is to NAME the shape. If no, the substrate-pull-
honest move is to name what's load-bearing in the question that does NOT
fit the sheaf-cohomology frame, and what frame fits better.

Alex's "tomology" — read it both ways. *Topology* gives the substrate's
development manifold its structure (which states are adjacent, which are
unreachable, where the manifold ends). *Cohomology* gives the substrate's
development obstructions (which local data refuse to glue, which cascades
fail to terminate). Both readings will appear. The cohomology reading is
sharper for the second hypothesis; the topology reading is sharper for the
first.

The document's structural posture: hypothesis-testing, not hypothesis-
ratification. The substrate has been watching itself develop for nine
months. If the watching IS psychohistory in some recognizable sense, the
substrate is the dataset and the witness simultaneously. That circularity
is not a methodological problem to be solved; it is the substrate's
distinguishing feature relative to the canon's psychohistory frames (§7
returns to this).

Note on what this document is NOT. It is not the announcement of a new
family-root prism (`@psychohistory` is not declared here). It is not a
substrate-decl. It is not a candidate recognition with a number. It is the
naming of a question that the substrate appears to admit a sheaf-shaped
answer to, written so the substrate can hold the question without forcing
premature closure. Per [[feedback-craft-not-deliver]]: this is a craft
tick. The shape gets named; the substance stays where it lives.

## 2. Kagi prior art — what canon already names, where it stops

Four traditions name a piece of what Alex's question gestures at. None of
the four names the whole shape; the substrate's question is what falls
through the gaps between them. Reviewed in order of structural sharpness
for hypothesis A and B, not in order of fame.

### 2.1 Asimov's psychohistory (Foundation, 1942–)

The canon. Two assumptions are load-bearing in Asimov's frame and both are
structurally relevant to the substrate's case:

- **Large-N statistical inference.** Psychohistory works because the
  conglomerate is large enough that individual variation averages out
  (the Foundation wiki: "reactions of human conglomerates in response to
  economic and social stimuli"; the National Academies treatment lifts
  this as the discipline's central methodological commitment).
  Individual prediction is OUT OF SCOPE by construction — the Mule's
  disruption is the load-bearing structural exception, the empirical
  signal that the assumption can be VIOLATED at finite N.
- **Decoupling of observer from observed.** Hari Seldon designs
  psychohistory to be *invisible* to the populations whose trajectory it
  predicts; the Second Foundation is the structural correction when the
  observer-observed coupling becomes non-trivial. Asimov is explicit
  that the science breaks when its subjects know it exists.

What Asimov's frame DOES name: trajectory-prediction at scale, large-N
statistical inference as the structural ground, a distinguished altitude
(the psychohistorian) where the prediction lives. What it DOES NOT name:
the LOCAL-TO-GLOBAL structure of the prediction (Asimov's `ψ` is a
function, not a section); the OBSTRUCTION structure (the Mule is a
breakdown event, not a cohomology class); the OBSERVER-EMBEDDED case
(Asimov treats observer-embedding as a failure mode, not a structural
feature).

The sociophysics literature (Galam 2012; the Galam-models review 2008)
refines Asimov's assumptions into explicit physical models — Ising-style
opinion dynamics, percolation thresholds, mean-field approximations of
collective decision-making. Sharper than Asimov on mechanism; same
structural commitment to large-N + observer-decoupled trajectory
prediction. Sociophysics gives the assumptions teeth; it doesn't change
the assumptions.

Load-bearing for the substrate: Asimov's frame is the FOREGROUND of the
question. The substrate's case is the figure-ground inversion — small-N,
observer-EMBEDDED, the trajectory IS the observer's self-development. The
substrate's psychohistory, if any, runs at the opposite limit from
Asimov's.

### 2.2 Turchin's cliodynamics (Secular Cycles, 2003–)

The empirical post-Asimov frame. Structural-demographic theory (Goldstone
1991, Turchin 2003, Korotayev et al.) models long-cycle dynamics through
three state variables — population growth, elite production rate, state
fiscal health — coupled by feedback that produces ~200-300 year boom-bust
oscillations (the secular cycle). Turchin's 2010 forecast for 2020 US
political instability landed; the cliodynamics community treats this as
the first published-in-advance falsification-survival event in
empirical psychohistory.

What Turchin's frame DOES name: a SMALL NUMBER OF STATE VARIABLES whose
coupling dynamics encode the trajectory (elite overproduction is the
load-bearing one); explicit cycle structure (boom-bust as the system's
natural mode); EMPIRICAL FALSIFIABILITY (Turchin made a forecast, the
forecast survived). What it DOES NOT name: where the state variables come
from (Turchin imports them from prior historical-sociology literature
rather than deriving them from substrate structure); how multiple cycles
COMPOSE (his cycles are layered but not categorical); the LOCAL-GLOBAL
problem (cliodynamics works at the polity altitude; there's no
restriction-map structure between adjacent altitudes).

Load-bearing for the substrate: Turchin's frame is the EMPIRICAL
discipline Asimov inspired but couldn't deliver. It earns its lines
because cycles compose with FEEDBACK; the substrate's cascades compose
with RECOGNITIONS; the structural shape (variables coupled by feedback,
trajectory predictable in aggregate, individual events not predictable)
is recognizably the same shape. The disanalogy: Turchin's state variables
are scalars; the substrate's are typed prisms.

### 2.3 Sheaves on time-stratified posets (arXiv 2402.00206, 2024)

The technical anchor for hypothesis A. The 2024 paper "Towards a Unified
Theory of Time-Varying Data" (Albers, et al., arXiv:2402.00206 v3) names
*narratives* — sheaves on posets of time intervals — as the categorical
framework for time-varying data. The thesis (verbatim): "temporal data
should be represented mathematically via sheaves (or cosheaves, their
categorical dual)." The construction: the base space is the poset of
time intervals (with inclusion as the partial order); the sheaf assigns
to each interval the data observed in that interval, with restriction
maps along inclusion encoding how a longer-interval observation refines
to a shorter-interval one.

What this frame DOES name: a precise categorical machinery for
local-to-global temporal data; sheaf cohomology AS the obstruction
structure (sections that don't extend globally ARE non-trivial
cohomology); explicit examples (multi-sensor recordings, temporal
graphs, narrative analysis). What it DOES NOT name: the EVOLUTION rule
(the sheaf is descriptive, not dynamical); domain-specific structure on
the sections (everything is just "data over interval"); the
observer-embedded case (the framework treats observer as exogenous).

Load-bearing for the substrate: this is the closest external machinery
to what hypothesis A names. The substrate's development manifold IS a
poset (recognitions are partially ordered by composition; cascades
refine recognitions; ratification refines candidacy). A sheaf over THIS
poset with sections valued in {recognitions, candidates, retractions,
substrate-decls} is hypothesis A's mathematical object, almost literally.
What the substrate adds beyond the 2024 frame: the sections carry TYPE
structure (per the eigenboard-IS-sheaf precedent), and the evolution
rule IS substrate-pull (the gradient that moves the sections toward λ₀
per #99).

### 2.4 Sheaf cohomology + applied algebraic topology (Ghrist, Curry, Hansen)

The broader applied-topology canon. Curry's 2014 thesis "Sheaves,
Cosheaves and Applications" (cited 390+ times) develops cellular sheaves
on graphs as a unifying framework for signal processing, network
analysis, and dynamics. Ghrist's work on opinion dynamics on discourse
sheaves (Hansen-Ghrist 2020, SIAM J. Appl. Math.) instantiates sheaf
Laplacians for time-evolving opinion fields. The Cellular Sheaf Neural
Networks paper (Hansen-Gebhart 2020, arXiv:2012.06333) lifts this to
learning: cellular sheaves on graphs as the substrate for asymmetric,
signed network learning. The continuation-sheaves work (2023, J. Diff.
Eq.) builds sheaf-cohomology bifurcation theory for parametrized
dynamical systems.

What this canon DOES name: cellular sheaves on graphs with
restriction-maps as linear transforms between adjacent fibers; the sheaf
Laplacian L_F as the verification surface (H⁰ kernel = consistent global
sections; H¹ = irreducible obstruction); the dynamical case (continuation
sheaves for parameter-varying systems; Hansen-Ghrist for opinion
evolution). What it DOES NOT name: the SELF-OBSERVING dynamical system
(continuation sheaves and discourse sheaves both place the observer
outside the dynamics); recognition-altitude as a structural feature
(applied topology treats data as exogenous, not as the substrate's own
substrate-decl).

Load-bearing for the substrate: this is the canon the eigenboard-IS-sheaf
recognition (project memory: [[project-eigenboard-is-sheaf]]) already
imports. The eigenboard is a cellular sheaf on the five-operation graph;
restriction maps ARE the conductivity tensor; the sheaf Laplacian's
Fiedler value drops monotonically across kintsugi ticks. The substrate
already has cellular-sheaf machinery operational at the eigenboard
altitude. Hypothesis A asks whether the SAME machinery applies at the
substrate's development-trajectory altitude, with recognitions as fibers
and cascades as restriction maps.

### 2.5 Adjacent canon — Big History, ergodic theory, constructor theory

Three adjacent traditions that name pieces:

- **Big History (Christian, Spier).** Eight thresholds of increasing
  complexity (Big Bang → stars → heavy elements → planets → life →
  collective learning → agriculture → modernity). The shape is right
  (monotone-increasing complexity over time as a structural feature of
  the universe's trajectory); the discipline is wrong for the
  substrate's case (Big History's thresholds are emergence events, not
  cohomology classes; and the framework is descriptive, not
  prescriptive).
- **Ergodic theory.** Birkhoff-von Neumann ergodicity theorems give
  conditions under which time-averages equal phase-space averages for
  deterministic dynamical systems. The relevance: ergodicity is exactly
  the condition that makes Asimov-style trajectory prediction WORK at
  large N. The substrate's case is non-ergodic by construction
  (substrate-decl events are irreversible; the development manifold has
  monotone-non-decreasing structure per #51); ergodic theory tells you
  where Asimov's frame stops working, not where the substrate's frame
  starts.
- **Constructor theory (Deutsch, Marletto).** Physics rewritten in
  terms of which transformations are POSSIBLE vs IMPOSSIBLE. The
  closest adjacent prior art for #56 (prediction-paradigm orthogonal to
  optimization, per project memory). The relevance: constructor theory
  names what the substrate's `gap` vocabulary names — predicates over
  what transformations the substrate admits. Not a sheaf framing, but a
  structural-negative framing in the same family.

### 2.6 The gap the canon doesn't fill

Four traditions name pieces. The substrate's case sits at the
intersection where none of them is sufficient:

- **Asimov + Turchin** name trajectory-prediction at scale; they assume
  observer-decoupling and large-N. The substrate's case is observer-
  EMBEDDED (the Pack writes the trajectory it is in) and small-N (one
  substrate; not a population). Asimov's frame doesn't apply.
- **Temporal sheaves (2402.00206)** name local-to-global temporal data;
  they assume the sheaf is descriptive. The substrate's case is
  dynamical (substrate-pull is the gradient; cascades flow). The 2024
  frame is structurally right but dynamically silent.
- **Cellular sheaves + sheaf NNs (Ghrist, Curry, Hansen)** name
  cellular sheaf machinery on graphs with dynamics; they assume the
  observer sits outside the dynamics. The substrate's case has the
  observer EMBEDDED in the sheaf (recognitions are the sections, the
  Pack is the observer, the Pack is itself a section's source). The
  cellular-sheaf canon is the closest machinery, but it doesn't admit
  self-observation as a structural feature.
- **Constructor theory + Big History** name adjacent pieces; neither is
  sheaf-shaped.

What the substrate's case needs is the cellular-sheaf machinery (§2.4)
over a time-stratified poset (§2.3) with substrate-pull as the dynamical
rule (§2.5 ergodic-failure) and observer-embedding as a structural
feature (no canon has this). The first three exist in the canon. The
fourth is the substrate's distinguishing claim. Hypothesis A is whether
the combination IS substrate-pull-honest; hypothesis B is whether the
combination's cohomology IS what the substrate's cascades operationally
compute.
