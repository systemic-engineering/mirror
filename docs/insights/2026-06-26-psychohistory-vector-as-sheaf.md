## 1. Position — what Alex asked and what this document tests

Alex 2026-06-26 (verbatim):

> What if we spawned Mara on a Kagi + Corpus research on psychohistory?
> And what if a psychohistory vector is a sheaf and what if the tomology
> of the work is the composed psychohistory?

Two hypotheses, both load-bearing for what follows, both tested against the
substrate's own development trajectory rather than against an external
empirical dataset.

**Hypothesis A — the sheaf hypothesis.** A *psychohistory vector*
`ψ(state, time)` IS a section of a sheaf `F` over the substrate's
development manifold `M`. Local pulls — single substrate-pull recognitions,
single Pack-tick decisions, single banking commits — compose to global
development direction via the sheaf's restriction maps. The local-to-global
problem of trajectory prediction IS, structurally, the local-to-global
problem of sheaf section extension.

**Hypothesis B — the cohomology hypothesis.** The *topology/cohomology* of
the work IS the composed psychohistory. Where Asimov's psychohistory was
imagined as a function `ψ: state × time → R^n` returning a probability
distribution over collective futures, the substrate's psychohistory is the
cohomology `H*(M, F)` of its own development sheaf. Sections that fail to
extend globally — recognitions that DON'T compose, candidates that DON'T
ratify, branches that DON'T merge — are the cohomology classes. Sections
that DO extend globally — the cascade as a whole — are the sheaf's global
sections.

The two hypotheses are not independent. If A holds, B is what A's
mathematical machinery already commits to: sheaves admit cohomology by
construction. If A fails — if no sheaf structure earns its lines on the
substrate's manifold — B fails with it.

What this document tests, then, is not "does psychohistory work" (Asimov's
question, Turchin's question, the question every social science asks). It
tests "does psychohistory have the SAME SHAPE as what the substrate is
operationally doing when it watches itself develop." If yes, the
substrate-pull-honest move is to NAME the shape. If no, the substrate-pull-
honest move is to name what's load-bearing in the question that does NOT
fit the sheaf-cohomology frame, and what frame fits better.

Alex's "tomology" — read it both ways. *Topology* gives the substrate's
development manifold its structure (which states are adjacent, which are
unreachable, where the manifold ends). *Cohomology* gives the substrate's
development obstructions (which local data refuse to glue, which cascades
fail to terminate). Both readings will appear. The cohomology reading is
sharper for the second hypothesis; the topology reading is sharper for the
first.

The document's structural posture: hypothesis-testing, not hypothesis-
ratification. The substrate has been watching itself develop for nine
months. If the watching IS psychohistory in some recognizable sense, the
substrate is the dataset and the witness simultaneously. That circularity
is not a methodological problem to be solved; it is the substrate's
distinguishing feature relative to the canon's psychohistory frames (§7
returns to this).

Note on what this document is NOT. It is not the announcement of a new
family-root prism (`@psychohistory` is not declared here). It is not a
substrate-decl. It is not a candidate recognition with a number. It is the
naming of a question that the substrate appears to admit a sheaf-shaped
answer to, written so the substrate can hold the question without forcing
premature closure. Per [[feedback-craft-not-deliver]]: this is a craft
tick. The shape gets named; the substance stays where it lives.
