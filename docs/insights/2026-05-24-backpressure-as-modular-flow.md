## The recognition

Mirror's Prism Bundle Tower (Fiber → Connection → Gauge → Transport → Closure) names the **algebraic** structure of operations. It has no **temporal** layer. Backpressure — the demand-flow handshake from Elixir's GenStage / Broadway, from Anna Jakobs's host-device OpenCL protocol (2012, FH Aachen / FZJ), from any well-engineered producer-consumer pipeline — IS the missing temporal layer.

Specifically: **backpressure is the discrete analog of the modular flow on the spectral triple.** When the consumer is "hot" (queue full, demand low), the backpressure signal cools the algebra — operations slow down. When the consumer is "cold" (queue empty, demand high), the backpressure signal heats the algebra — operations accelerate. The system settles at the KMS condition. That's not metaphor; it's the same operator (`D`) acting on the same Hilbert space (`H`), now with its time-evolution (`σ_t`) made explicit.

The Bundle Tower says what mirror's operations *are*. The Scheduler layer says when they *run*. Together they're the complete spectral triple at the runtime layer: geometry plus dynamics.

## The spectral-triple connection

Per the Dirac-operator-on-graphs insight (`~/dev/systemic.engineering/practice/insights/spectral-db/dirac-operator-on-graphs.md` §6):

> *"A state ω on the algebra A is thermal (KMS) at inverse temperature β if ω(ab) = ω(b σ_{iβ}(a)) where σ_t is the modular flow generated by D."*

The modular flow σ_t generated by D is the canonical time-evolution of operators on the algebra. For finite graphs, σ_t is trivial as a flow — but the KMS condition still gives a meaningful notion of *equilibrium vs. non-equilibrium* for how operations interact with state. Mirror's runtime, treated as a state on the operator algebra, has a KMS condition: producer-rate and consumer-rate balance at the temperature set by the demand handshake.

Backpressure makes the equilibrium computable. Without it, the system has no thermal regulation — producers run as fast as they can; consumers either absorb or drop. With it, the producer's rate is determined by the consumer's announced capacity — the demand signal IS the temperature.

**Concretely:** the temperature `β` becomes the demand-queue depth. High `β` (cold, slow equilibration) = small demand window, careful pacing. Low `β` (hot, fast equilibration) = wide demand window, aggressive throughput. The system finds its operating temperature at the balance between production and consumption.

## gen_prism already IS a Stage

Mirror's actor model is already content-addressed and Stage-shaped. From `boot/std/mirror/runtime/gen_prism.mirror`:

> *"A gen_prism is a name. The name points at a ref. The ref points at a crystal. The crystal is the current state. Sending a message reads the crystal, computes a new crystal, and advances the ref."*
>
> *"A tick is a function: (state, message) → (state, emissions). Pure. Replayable. Reproducible."*

Map that onto GenStage's vocabulary:

| GenStage / Broadway | mirror gen_prism |
|---|---|
| Stage | gen_prism |
| State machine | the crystal chain |
| `handle_events(events, from, state)` | `tick(state, message) → tick_result` |
| Demand | (missing today) |
| `handle_demand(n, state)` | (missing today) |
| Emit events downstream | `emissions: [json]` |
| Producer / Consumer / ProducerConsumer | role flag (missing today) |
| Dispatcher (round-robin / partitioned / broadcast) | (missing today) |
| Subscription topology | (missing today, but composable via send/call) |

The pure operations are there. **What's missing is the demand contract** — the explicit signal from a consumer gen_prism to a producer gen_prism saying "I can take N more messages." Without it, `send` is fire-and-forget; with it, `send` becomes demand-aware and the pipeline self-regulates.

This isn't a new abstraction. It's an extension to `gen_prism` of one optional field (`demand_window`) and one optional callback (`on_demand`). Existing gen_prisms that don't need backpressure ignore both. New gen_prisms that participate in pipelines opt in. The grammar grows by ~15 lines.

## Why GenStage / Broadway is the right protocol prior art

GenStage (Elixir, 2016) is the canonical demand-driven flow protocol on the BEAM. Broadway (2019) is its production-grade specialization for data ingestion. Both were designed by José Valim and his team specifically to solve the producer-overrun problem that classical actor-model systems suffer.

Key properties that transfer to mirror:

1. **Demand is explicit and bounded.** Consumers ask for N events; producers send up to N; no buffer overflow possible. The protocol enforces backpressure structurally.
2. **Backpressure propagates upstream.** If a slow consumer reduces its demand, that reduction flows back through every intermediate stage. Slow operations naturally throttle their producers.
3. **Multiple dispatcher strategies.** Round-robin (fair share), partitioned (sticky routing), broadcast (fan-out). Each strategy preserves backpressure semantics; the dispatcher is policy, not mechanism.
4. **Subscription topology is first-class.** Pipelines are declared, not assembled imperatively. The shape of the topology determines the system's behavior under load.
5. **Failure handling is structural.** Producer crashes propagate to consumers via the subscription; consumer crashes free up demand at producers. The system degrades predictably.

Mirror's gen_prism inherits content-addressing on top of GenStage's demand-flow. That's the unique contribution: every state in the pipeline is hashable, every message is hashable, every transformation is replayable. GenStage gives us *self-regulating flow*; mirror's content-addressing gives us *time-traveling debugging* and *deterministic replay* over the same flow.

## Why Gleam's `stage_play` matters

Elixir's GenStage uses dynamic typing — you can connect any Stage to any Stage and find out at runtime whether the message types match. Gleam (statically-typed BEAM language) faces the same constraint mirror does: **how do you express GenStage-shape pipelines when the types must be checked at compile time?**

`stage_play` (Alex's Gleam exploration, `/Users/alexwolf/dev/projects/stage_play/`) is the prior art for that translation. Whatever pattern it lands on for typing producer-consumer subscriptions is directly applicable to mirror's gen_prism extension: mirror is also statically typed at the grammar level, also wants compile-time composition checks, also runs over a BEAM-shaped runtime semantically (the gen_prism crystal chain IS BEAM-shaped, just content-addressed).

## Apple UMA + Anna's shared-memory bus

For mirror's CPU/GPU boundary specifically (when the Scheduler Tower mediates between LapackBackend and MetalBackend), the channel IS shared memory:

- **Apple Silicon UMA** makes the channel zero-copy. CPU writes; GPU reads; same physical memory. The backpressure signal is queue depth; the data flows without buffer movement.
- **Anna's VBO + OpenCL interop** (Jakobs 2012 §4.4, §7.2.1) is the equivalent for separate-memory architectures: a shared buffer between OpenGL rendering and OpenCL computation. The host explicitly synchronizes; the backpressure signal is buffer-write completion.
- **OpenCL command queues** (Jakobs 2012 §3) ARE the backpressure mechanism at the OpenCL layer: the device's queue depth limits how much work is in flight; submitting more blocks until the queue drains.

The Scheduler Tower's CPU/GPU specialization isn't a new design — it's the GenStage protocol implemented over a shared-memory bus instead of BEAM mailboxes. Anna's thesis is the architectural reference for the bus; GenStage is the protocol reference for the demand handshake.

## What this enables concretely

- **Parallel batched hashing.** N SpectralCoordinates computed by a GPU producer Stage; CPU consumer reads results at its rate; demand flow auto-balances.
- **Kintsugi loop as a pipeline.** Parse Stage → render Stage → diff Stage → loss Stage → next iteration. Each runs on its preferred device with backpressure between stages.

- **Mirror's compiler gets a temperature.** A single scalar parameter β controls how aggressively the system settles. High β = explore widely (high entropy, low precision). Low β = settle tightly (low entropy, high precision). The KMS condition gives the natural equilibrium. Temporal twin of the spectral action that the kintsugi loss function already names spatially.

## Honest scoping

This is **v1.5**, not v1.

- **v1 (current):** CPU/LAPACK, single-threaded numerical ops, no GPU. Mirror's corpus is small; backpressure not yet relevant. gen_prism stays as-is; the Bundle Tower without a Scheduler Tower handles this fine.
- **v1.5 (when MetalBackend lands):** The Scheduler Tower becomes structurally needed because GPU dispatch without backpressure misbehaves under any non-trivial load (driver-level kernel queuing eventually OOMs). The demand-contract extension to gen_prism is the load-bearing piece.
- **v2 (when distributed spectral-db lands):** Scheduler Tower extends to handle network-shaped backpressure. Same protocol; bus is the network. spectral-db's distribution layer consumes the Scheduler Tower.

Naming the recognition now matters because it changes how v1's gen_prism evolution should be designed — keep the demand-contract slot open in the type so v1.5's extension is additive, not breaking.
